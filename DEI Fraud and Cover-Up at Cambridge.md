“Wouldn’t it be the most ridiculous story ever told, if the boy who couldn’t read and write at 18 gets a PhD before he’s 30?”

Those are the [words](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMotp-PzBAI) of Jason Arday—the 2002 Professorial Chair in the Sociology of Education at Cambridge University. Indeed, the story of his metamorphosis from arguably the most disadvantaged person in the UK to elite scholar-athlete-philanthropist would make even Walt Disney raise an eyebrow.

Born to Ghanaian parents, Arday grew up on a council estate (the British equivalent of Section 8 housing) in south London. At age three he was [diagnosed](https://www.ljmu.ac.uk/about-us/bicentenary/our-people/professor-jason-arday/professor-jason-arday-profile) with global development delay and autism. Therapists [said](https://news.educ.cam.ac.uk/230223-jason-arday) he would be confined to an assisted living facility. Miraculously, at 11 years old, he spoke his first word (“hello”). When he was 18, he learned to read and write.

Under the [influence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMotp-PzBAI) of a white high school teacher who “gave \[him\] agency,” Arday pursued higher education. After receiving a PhD from Liverpool John Moores University, he rocketed up the academic ladder as a scholar of racism, education, and inequality. In three years he went from senior lecturer at Roehampton (2018) to associate professor at Durham (2019) to professor at Glasgow (2021). Ivy League universities (plural) [tried to recruit him](https://abcnews.com/GMA/Living/meet-cambridges-youngest-black-professor-jason-arday/story?id=97938873), but he had his heart set on Oxford or Cambridge. In 2023, at age 37, he became the youngest black full professor in the history of Cambridge. He is one of the youngest people of any race to ever hold a chair professorship at Oxbridge.

In his spare time, [Arday ran](https://www.aru.ac.uk/graduation-and-alumni/honorary-award-holders2/jason-arday) 30 marathons in 35 days and 300 miles in three days.

As of 2023, he had worked with [“more than 70” charities](https://abcnews.com/GMA/Living/meet-cambridges-youngest-black-professor-jason-arday/story?id=97938873) and raised [more than £5 million](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2023/jul/11/jason-arday-cambridge-university-youngest-black-professor).

Up until his appointment at Cambridge, Arday continued to face unspecified hardships. As [reported](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2023/jul/11/jason-arday-cambridge-university-youngest-black-professor) by the *Guardian* in 2023, despite previously being a professor at Durham and Glasgow, “He has barely slept over the past 15 years..., working through most nights—often on his academic and charity work, but sometimes just to make ends meet.”

He has received “ [hundreds of thousands](https://www.thebookseller.com/author-interviews/jason-arday-on-his-remarkable-journey-from-non-verbal-child-to-cambridge-professor) of letters and emails” from fans [“all over the world.”](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2023/jul/11/jason-arday-cambridge-university-youngest-black-professor) This indicates a level of fame [comparable](https://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-21835118) to an upper-middle-tier household name celebrity.

But how much of “the most ridiculous story ever told” is *true*?

I was recently contacted by a faculty member at Cambridge (whose name I will not reveal) who alleges that Arday is a plagiarist and fabulist. My source claims that many if not most professors at Cambridge have known about this for more than a year. However, Arday and/or Cambridge’s administration are using intimidation and legal threats to squelch the story. As far as I am aware, the only mention of Arday’s alleged plagiarism that exists in the public domain is on the personal website of retired Plymouth Marjon University professor Dave Harris. (More on that later.)

Besides the plagiarism, there are serious questions about Arday’s biographical claims.

Developing normal speaking abilities after being nonverbal until age 11 is medically [improbable](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/cogbehavneurol/fulltext/10.1097/wnn.0b013e318190d185~speech-acquisition-in-older-nonverbal-individuals-with).

Arday has repeatedly [made](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMotp-PzBAI) [unequivocal](https://rulingpassions.wordpress.com/2022/03/22/podcast-launch-episode-2/) [assertions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FA78tV8lar8) that he could not read, write, or even [spell his own name](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DI4TuXZODng/) until he was 18. But he left secondary school with General Certificate of Secondary Education (GCSE) qualifications in Physical Education and Textiles. Despite being in vocational subjects, these tests require literacy, and they are normally taken at age 15 or 16. Based on [publicly available information](https://www.stmarys.ac.uk/news/2023/st-marys-alumnus-becomes-youngest-ever-black-professor-at-cambridge-university), it is possible to recreate a timeline that places Arday’s test date before age 18. It is conceivable that he received “special arrangements” to allow him to take the test with help from a reader and scribe. However, a [write-up](https://www.thebookseller.com/author-interviews/jason-arday-on-his-remarkable-journey-from-non-verbal-child-to-cambridge-professor) of his [forthcoming memoir](https://www.simonandschuster.co.uk/books/Great-and-Unfortunate-Things/Jason-Arday/9781398542747) says that he “left secondary school with two GCSEs to his name, still *barely* able to read or write.” So he may be backing away from his original story.

30 marathons in 35 days and 300 miles in three days are feats of dubious believability. Wikipedia [lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Karnazes) running 50 marathons in 50 days as one of the highlights of professional ultramarathon runner Dean Karnazes’s career. 30 in 35 days is *possible* for an amateur, but only with [intensive training](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-16-112.xml-Fulltext) and planning. 300 miles in three days is an even bigger stretch. For comparison, Yiannis Kouros, who has been described as “the greatest ultrarunner of all time,” set a [world record](https://ultrarunninghistory.com/yiannis-kouros/) in 1984 when he ran 359 miles in three days. Arday asserts that he was able to do all this because “If you have the will, it will always supersede skill”—a proposition that anyone with experience in athletics knows to be false.

However, it is impossible to prove or disprove Arday’s biographical claims without more direct evidence. Therefore, I will only address questions about his scholarship.

Here I present evidence that, starting with his PhD dissertation, Arday routinely passes off the words and ideas of others as his own. If one removes the apparently plagiarized material from his record, there is little or no real scholarship. I discuss the cover-up by senior administrators at Cambridge and the broader implications for free speech and DEI ideology.

### The Dissertation

I ran [Arday’s 2015 PhD dissertation](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/4552/1/158222_Jason%20Arday_%20Final%20PhD%20Thesis%20Vesrion%20Final%20Draft%20Oct%202015.pdf) through the plagiarism-detection program Copyleaks. It found substantial overlap with previously published work, particularly a [dissertation submitted by Paula Zwozdiak-Myers in 2009](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/4316/1/FulltextThesis.pdf). (Zwozdiak-Myers received her degree from Brunel University London, where she is currently a Reader in Education.) The chart below compares passages from Arday (2015) and Zwozdiak-Myers (2009). From literally the first page of the Introduction to his dissertation (which starts on page 17 of the manuscript), Arday relies heavily on Zwozdiak-Myers for key ideas and arguments. Many passages are lifted with minimal editing, sometimes retaining copy-editing mistakes from the original source.

Arday appears to borrow from other sources without attribution. I won’t go through all of the examples detected by Copyleaks, but here are two more.

First, he copies and paraphrases from an [obscure article](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ964960.pdf) by Muhammad Athar Hussain et al. published in the *Turkish Online Journal of Distance Education* in 2011.

### The 10 Most Important Publications

Dave Harris is a retired Professor of Leisure and Education at Plymouth Marjon University. He hosts a website that provides free teaching materials.

Harris [writes](https://www.arasite.org/Ardaypage.html) that he first read Arday’s work with the intention of summarizing the arguments to make them accessible. However, what he euphemistically refers to as “oddities” were “so frequent that they dominated \[his\] reading.” Harris emailed Arday, pointing out examples and asking if he could provide clarification.

Arday replied to Harris as follows: “Please do not email me again. Should you have the time to spare, I invite you to direct your resources elsewhere. Anything further from you will be considered bullying and harassment.”

After Harris and his colleague prodded several journal editors and administrators at Cambridge, two journals issued corrections to Arday’s publications.

### The Cover-Up

Jason Arday was hired by Cambridge explicitly to advance the cause of racial diversity. When he joined the university in 2023, the [official announcement](https://news.educ.cam.ac.uk/230223-jason-arday) included the following quote by Pro-Vice-Chancellor for Education Bhaskar Vira:

> Jason Arday is an exceptional scholar of race, inequality and education. He will contribute significantly to Cambridge’s research in this area and to addressing the under-representation of people from socio-economically disadvantaged backgrounds: especially those from Black, Asian, and other Minority Ethnic communities....We are making extensive efforts in this regard—including the expansion of the ‘Get In Cambridge’ programme which provides financial support to address systemic disadvantages and the lack of social and educational capital which often constrains the ability of minority ethnic students to continue with their education. A great deal remains to be done and Jason’s expertise will inform and enhance these ongoing endeavours.

(Readers of this Newsletter may remember Vira as one of the administrators who [tried to get me fired](https://ncofnas.com/p/cambridge-universitys-war-on-free) from Cambridge in 2024 for writing about race and affirmative action.)

The head of the Faculty of Education, Hilary Cremin, told the *Times* of London that “hiring Arday was proof of the university’s commitment to tackling existing inequalities.” She provided the following [statement](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/education/article/jason-arday-cambridge-university-youngest-black-professor-2023-r2vm7dtfn):

> Not only is Jason Arday a world-class scholar, he also brings a wealth of experience that can help us to take forward our efforts to widen participation at all levels. Hopefully it is becoming increasingly clear that with concerted effort and a genuine commitment to change, those barriers that still exist either to working or studying at top universities can be overcome.

Arday [stated](https://news.educ.cam.ac.uk/230223-jason-arday) that he was coming to Cambridge to “open doors” to members of underrepresented groups:

> My work focuses primarily on how we can open doors to more people from disadvantaged backgrounds and truly democratise higher education. Hopefully being in a place like Cambridge will provide me with the leverage to lead that agenda nationally and globally.

In other words, hiring Arday was an overtly political act. Everyone involved—including Arday himself—understood that he was being recruited to promote the ideology of DEI and increase the representation of certain groups.

You can imagine how Vira, Cremin, and others felt when in June 2023 they received an email from Dave Harris’s colleague alerting them to Arday’s alleged plagiarism. From the perspective of the university (represented by Rhys Morgan), any pre-2023 plagiarism was irrelevant. It is difficult to understand this position. Arday was hired on the basis of his work. Surely the university would wish to know whether or how much of this work was fraudulent.

### Free Speech and DEI

There are more than a thousand black people in the UK with IQs over 140 who would be capable of doing serious work in the sociology of education. If Cambridge administrators wanted a black professor to serve as a role model for underrepresented minorities, they could have found someone qualified. Instead, they grabbed a random black person with an unvetted fairytale backstory and almost nothing that even resembles genuine scholarship. When evidence of the black person’s long history of plagiarism is brought to their attention, they excuse it on the basis that he was the victim of “direct and indirect racism.” In their view, he cannot be asked to follow the same rules that we expect schoolchildren to understand. I know it sounds silly to say that “leftists are the real racists.” But by hiring Jason Arday and refusing to investigate credible accusations against him, isn’t Cambridge making a statement that the only way to have black professors is to abandon all standards?

There is a conflict between what universities such as Cambridge call “DEI” and something else that they claim to value, namely, free speech. DEI demands that we accept the fiction that people like Jason Arday, who are hired because they tick certain identity and ideology boxes, are just as qualified as scholars selected on the basis of academic merit. But if people are allowed to speak freely, they might point out that this is false. The Cambridge administration resolves the dilemma by siding with DEI against free speech. Faculty know what is [likely to happen to them](https://ncofnas.com/p/cambridge-universitys-war-on-free) if they speak about the Arday situation. That is why the administration has been able to bury the story for three years.

The upshot is that we lose both free speech *and* equality. Equality means treating people the same without regard to immutable characteristics like race, and that is not what is happening at Cambridge.
