I had just heard from a source (and it’s now been confirmed in other [news](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/governance/accreditation/2026/09/25/accreditation-panel-recommends-suspending-apa-punts-aba)) that the American Psychological Association (APA) may lose its accreditor status for clinical psychology programs. The National Advisory Committee on Institutional Quality and Integrity, a federal advisory board, recommended suspending the APA’s accreditor status for 1 year. According to news reports, the recommendation will now go to Department of Education Under Secretary Nicholas Kent for a final decision.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wdrW!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F44c46377-4d45-41ce-aea5-6c1e4646adfe_1408x768.jpeg)

The APA had been accused of antisemitism as well as various other biases. As I’ve noted, [elsewhere](https://www.christopherjferguson.com/Men%20and%20Boys%20Guidelines.pdf), for instance, the APA’s practice guidelines for men and boys were widely perceived as anti-male, and certainly derogatory of traditional men and families. The advisory group had concerns also about programs advancing fringe “decolonial therapy,” and other ideological approaches. Also there were concerns about the behavior of some specific programs with George Washington University being mentioned. Indeed one of my own [undergraduate students](https://authorinquestion.substack.com/p/rupture?r=92g2kn&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) has written a substack detailing his mistreatment at that university. I think he’s handled that almost better than I have, as I found their treatment of him remarkably unprofessional. The general gist was that clinical training in psychology had become rubbish under the APA’s watch and, I suspect, too often that is probably true.

I’ve been a critic of the APA for many years for many reasons (which boil down to my viewing them as a selfish anti-science professional guild who has misinformed the public on science many times [^1]). I think it is clear they really need to reconsider and reform not only how they approach clinical training, but also recommit to a politically neutral science perspective, which they have never done during my professional lifetime.

What will come of this remains unclear. Given this is the Trump administration, my guess is the APA can expect no mercy there. But they may try to work out some kind of self-regulation deal to avoid suspension. They had already [stripped](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/03/27/health/psychology-dei-apa-trump.html) out some DEI requirements to appease the Trump administration last year.

Trump will only be in office for 2.5 more years. We could see yet another reversal under a Democrat administration eager to appease progressive voters. It would be nice if politics or the profession itself could settle on reasonable, data-driven, politically neutral guidelines, rather than see-sawing every 4 to 8 years. But alas, I also wish for a pony for Christmas.

[^1]: To be fair, and in full disclosure, I remain an associate editor at one of their journals. I’ve enjoyed my experience there, working with a fine editorial team. But it’s also a very siloed experience and I have nothing to do with the larger APA organization (I don’t even go to their conferences anymore). I [resigned](https://medium.com/@cjfergus/the-american-psychological-association-no-longer-represents-good-science-or-clinical-practice-an-9fb967cab106) my fellow status with the APA in frustration with the larger organization years ago.
