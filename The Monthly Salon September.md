### Wendell Berry: In Memoriam

The great Wendell Berry has died. ‘Great’ is probably an overused word, but in this case I would say it is justified. Berry was a remarkable man. A very particular figure whose influence was genuinely global. A Kentucky farmer who looked always to his place and to his past, and made of those materials something universal: something that would speak to people like me, and perhaps you, about what it means to be human in a time like this.

I spent much of yesterdy writing [an appreciation of Wendell’s life and work](https://firstthings.com/wendell-berrys-eternal-story/) for *First Things*. There will be many more of these in the coming days, I’m sure, from people who have engaged more deeply with him and his writing over the years. For now, here, I just want to mark and mourn his passing, on Monday, at the age of 92, in the Kentucky town he came from and lived in, and made famous. I was lucky enough to visit the place last autumn, when I gave the second [Wendell Berry Lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZTlM-6nZNdE) in his local church in Port Royal. Beforehand, I was a slightly nervous dinner guest of Wendell’s daughter Mary and her husband, Wendell himself, and his wife Tanya. It was an occasion I won’t forget.

A writer can sometimes identify obvious influences on his work, while others are more obscure; a mystery even to him. I have no difficulty identifying Wendell as one of mine. Why would it be that a boy from an English suburb would become interested in the agrarian writings of a Kentucky farmer? Apart from his sheer talent and drive - he wrote novels, essays and poems right up to his death, in intimidating numbers - I suppose it was the way he wrote about place, belonging and nature, and the vital place those things play in what it means to be human. This always spoke to me, from across the wide ocean. He wrote, too, about the powerful forces at play which seek to strip all these things away from us, which is what alerted me to his writing back in the 1990s, and made me feel some sense of solidarity and kinship with this man from another time and place.

Nearly ten years ago, I edited a collection of Wendell’s essays for Penguin, *[The World-Ending Fire](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/297824/the-world-ending-fire-by-berry-wendell/9780141984131)*[,](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/297824/the-world-ending-fire-by-berry-wendell/9780141984131) which remains the only author-approved selection of his work on sale. You can read the introduction to that book, and one of Wendell’s essays included in it, [here](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2017-02-09/the-world-ending-fire/). You can also, if you like, listen to a [programme that Wendell and I recorded with Andrew Marr on BBC Radio 4 back in 2017](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b08njtjg), where we discussed the book, his essays and his life’s work.

Pray for Wendell and his family today, if you can. But remember his legacy too - and see what you can do to live it out. The [Berry Center](https://berrycenter.org/), down in Kentucky, will be continuing to do just that.

### A word about paywalls

Finally, before I open the floor to you, my readers, to talk about any subject you like, a quick word about mammon, and its relationship to my writing here.

At the beginning of this year, I decided I would make all my writing here free for the foreseeable future. There were a couple of reasons for this: one practical, one principled. The practical reason was that I was quite ill for many months, and was writing very little here. I didn’t want the pressure of producing ‘content’ for people who had paid for it, and it wasn’t fair to be charing for very little. More recently, as I wrote here a month ago, I have felt a call to silence - or, at least, away from a heavy and constant engagement with words.

The more principled reason for this decision was that I feel increasingly uncomfortable charging for any writing that is focused on Christianity. I write for a living, and I have to feed my children, but I still feel I didn’t ought to be asking for money for people to access writing about the Christian path. If my writing on that subject is of any use at all, I feel I ought to offer it as a gift. In exchange, I have been asking for donations if people can afford them.

I intend to continue on this path. A few days ago, though - right after I had written about silence, in fact - I woke up early with some words in my head, and I had to write them down. My little essay [The Trap](https://paulkingsnorth.substack.com/p/the-trap) poured out of me, and it was clear that this piece needed to be paywalled - for reasons which become clear in the article itself. The dawn of AI, you see, introduces another dilemma that writers have never had to face before: everything we write which is publicly available on the Internet will be ‘scraped’ and fed into the AI training runs. Even articles about why AI is a bad thing will be used to train the AIs.

This is a lot to navigate, and I have realised that setting hard and fast rules just invites an opportunity to break them or see them made quickly obsolete. Still, we have to start somewhere. I have to keep this space available to write anything I like, whenever it comes up. I never know quite what that will be. But I am going to navigate these various thorny byways for now by doing three things:

- Firstly, any Christian writing on here will continue to be offered for free. If readers are able to make a small [donation](https://buymeacoffee.com/paulkingsnorth), or take out a paid subscription, that would be very helpful, but it is in no way required or expected.
- Any other writing - such as my recent surprise essay - may or may not be paywalled. I still want to make as much of my writing as I can freely available, but I reserve the right to paywall some of it, if only to keep the Eye of Sauron at bay for a few more years.
- Finally, I will soon be launching my own little home publisher, which will be putting out my writings on actual paper and sending them to readers through the post. This may develop, down the line, into a real-world newsletter, and maybe into publishing work by others too. We’ll see. To start with though, it will be an attempt to ground some of my writing in reality again. There will be more news on this soon.

I hope that all makes sense. For now, this is the basis on which I intend to publish my work here, for as long as I continue to do so.

And with that, readers, it is over to you to introduce any subject you’d like to talk about. Maybe you have some thoughts about Wendell Berry, or maybe you’d like to talk about something else entirely. The world is your oyster. Take the floor.
