[Ford Motor](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/F) [F 1.70%increase; green up pointing triangle](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/F) has told car dealers it intends to produce a four-door, gas-powered version of the Mustang for the first time, adding a variant to the “pony car” that has sold in the millions since 1964, according to people familiar with the matter.

The four-door Mustang was shown at a dealer event this week, the people said. It could go on sale before the close of this decade and resembles the current two-door Mustang, one of the people said. Two of the people said the car would have hybrid power; one of the people said it would be a hybrid V-8.

“We do not comment or speculate about future products,” a Ford spokesman said.

Ford had previously shown a version of the four-door vehicle at an internal event in June, according to another person familiar with the matter.

Product plans can change before production. Ford in recent years has canceled other future models, [including an electric SUV](https://www.wsj.com/business/autos/ford-cancels-plans-for-electric-suv-44817367?mod=article_inline) with three rows of seats.

The move to expand the normally two-door Mustang—one of the most famous American cars of all time and a fixture of pop culture and motor sports—comes as Ford seeks to double down on what it calls [its most “passionate” vehicles](https://www.wsj.com/business/autos/ford-cars-suvs-sports-cars-picksups-867a00fc?mod=article_inline).

Yet Ford has been increasingly interested in sedans, a vehicle category that [Detroit automakers have ceded](https://www.wsj.com/business/autos/sedans-cars-trucks-detroit-afb034ee?mod=article_inline) to foreign competitors such as [Toyota](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/JP/XTKS/7203) and [Honda](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/JP/XTKS/7267). Ford has been looking at how to re-enter the sedan segment while ensuring the vehicles make money, Chief Executive Jim Farley said earlier this year.

The original Mustang was so popular with its then-young baby boomer audience that it launched an entire generation of similar American performance cars. The Mustang has also been notable for outlasting its many competitors. The Chevrolet Camaro was discontinued in 2023, and the Dodge Charger and Challenger disappeared from the market for decades before their 2000s revival.

Dodge-parent [Stellantis](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/STLA) then relaunched the Charger last year as a gas-powered model, after introducing a poor-selling all-electric Charger in 2024. The Charger comes in two-door and four-door versions.

Through the decades, the Mustang nameplate has always been applied to two-door coupes and convertibles, save for some internal prototypes and design studies that Ford never put into production. The lone exception has been the [Mustang Mach-E](https://www.wsj.com/tech/personal-tech/best-evs-under-60000-dollars-3eed42f9?mod=article_inline), a performance-focused electric crossover SUV that Ford counts as a separate model.

The Mustang remains the world’s bestselling sports car. But like many such vehicles, the Mustang has seen its sales decline in recent years as buyers shift to more practical vehicles.

Ford sold more than 122,000 Mustangs in the U.S. in 2015, compared with about 45,000 last year. The current Mustang lineup ranges from a four-cylinder coupe that starts at about $33,000 to a high-end performance coupe that tops $100,000.

Adding a four-door version could broaden the model’s appeal to a wider audience, including families seeking more space than the coupe can offer. Other automakers have branched out in similar ways in recent decades. Porsche, a brand famous for its sports cars, now counts SUVs as its top-selling models.
