President Trump’s no-notice ceasefire declaration has upended the operational picture, and this week’s edition unpacks what it actually means — and what it doesn’t. Sal Mercogliano and Rain Waters rejoin Mooch to break down the geopolitical implications and whether this signals a genuine path to ending the war with Iran or another tactical pause that leaves CENTCOM in limbo.

Also this week, three-time Moochie winner Justin Bronk is back to trace how the air war over Ukraine has evolved from conventional fighter engagements into a conflict dominated by drones and ballistic missiles — and whether those lessons are migrating to other theaters. And Greg Jaffe’s *New York Times* deep dive into how Admiral Daryl Caudle landed the CNO job raises hard questions about what happens when the Pentagon’s senior promotion process gets bypassed in favor of loyalty tests and anti-woke litmus screens. Hegseth’s pattern of firings and list-scrubbing is now well documented; whether it’s producing a more lethal force or hollowing out the bench is the debate worth having.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tNjUTfwb_qY)

In this episode Mooch talks to longtime friend and media colleague *New York Times* military reporter Greg Jaffe who wrote about how Adm. Daryl Caudle reached out to then-Secretary of Defense nominee Pete Hegseth with an offer to help him prepare for his congressional testimony. The offer wasn't taken up, but Caudle was pitched to Hegseth's staff as a "warfighter who wanted to remove woke barriers."  
  
Hegseth had made no secret about his desire to fire, among others, the Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Lisa Frachetti, and once he was confirmed he did just that. The Navy forwarded three names as possible CNO replacements, but the list didn't include Caudle. That didn't dissuade Secretary Hegseth and he picked him anyway based on the fact Tim Palatore, his "special advisor," had convinced him Caudle was the best admiral to carry out the anti-woke agenda.  
  
Jaffe documents other firings and removal from promotion lists Hegseth has done based on the perception of being woke or not fully supporting the Pentagon's new warrior ethos agenda.  
  
Is this shunting of the peer review process what's needed to get the military back to core competency or a reckless waste of senior officer talent and contributions?  
  
Read Greg's story on this subject [here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/26/us/politics/caudle-navy-hegseth-parlatore-promotion.html) (subscription required).
