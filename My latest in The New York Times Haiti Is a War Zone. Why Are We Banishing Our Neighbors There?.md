Disaster management is something I take seriously, both as a retired U.S. Army General and as someone who still lives with the memory of Hurricane Katrina. Many people here in the United States remember my work in New Orleans after Katrina made landfall more than 20 years ago this summer. But my work in disaster management and recovery never truly ended.

To this day, I continue to work as a volunteer with multiple organizations focused on disaster management around the world. One such undertaking that is particularly close to my heart is collaborating with the Haitian diaspora here in the United States to help restore order and stability on the ground in Haiti.

In case you missed it, [I recently published an article in The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/23/opinion/trump-haiti-tps-immigration.html) about what the administration is currently doing to strip temporary protected status (TPS) from Haitians living and working in the U.S. As I argue in the piece, this will harm both the U.S. and Haiti – to say nothing of the individuals who would be left unemployed and stranded in an unsafe and unstable country.

The [full piece is available here from The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/23/opinion/trump-haiti-tps-immigration.html). I invite you to read it and I look forward to reading your thoughts.
