That this stuff is out is probably the biggest deterrent to it happening. Many of the people in Data Republican’s records are probably under surveillance now, and possibly already named in sealed indictments. There’s also a “watch what you wish for because you might get it” angle to this: The Insurrection Act is powerful medicine and while it might play badly politically, the President’s powers under it are immense, and many Presidential powers — including deciding in the first instance which of two state governments is recognized as legitimate — are not [subject](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/48/1/) to judicial review. Blue state governors might find themselves opposed by a new government in their state, loyal to the United States, that claims to be the legitimate government of the state and is recognized as such. (I’ve even [suggested](https://ir.law.utk.edu/utklaw_facpubs/191/) that something like this could lead to California splitting into two ore more states, following the precedent of West Virginia.)

Note that the plan is not for a coup, which [as I’ve suggested](https://ir.law.utk.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1185&context=utklaw_facpubs) is impractical in the United States: “In the classic coup d’état the nation wakes up one morning to hear that the political leadership has been arrested or co-opted, while the radio and television stations are under the control of the new regime. The civil servants go to work as usual, just following orders from a new batch of superiors. In the face of such a *fait accompli,* few are inclined to resist, particularly if, as is usually the case, the old regime wasn’t overly popular anyway.” A color revolution is not a coup, but it’s not really a civil war either — or at least it’s not meant to be. (I have some concerns that an attempted color revolution might lead to a civil war.)

But successful color revolutions turn out big crowds on one side only. In the United States, that’s not likely to happen. Aside from the military, President Trump has many loyalists. They might turn out on their own, or they might be invited. He could probably have as many as a million (possibly armed) supporters out in the streets overnight if he asked in the face of hostile mobs. Maybe more. Based on the experience with Tea Party rallies and Red state disaster responses, they would probably come with surprisingly competent logistical, medical. and communications support.

The results of that would probably be bloody, and more like a civil war than anything. Would Trump do that? Lincoln was willing to fight a civil war to keep the Union together and put down a Democratic insurrection. I doubt Trump would do less.

I hope that all of this is just pre-election vaporware and none of it will come to pass. But it’s worth noting that we’re talking about this because a group of 1500 senior government, military, and NGO officials have been talking about overthrowing the government among themselves. That alone is a cataclysmic change in our political environment. Many of them are likely prosecutable for seditious conspiracy — which unlike most other conspiracy crimes does not require an overt act in support of the conspiracy to constitute a crime — and even if, as is most likely, this whole thing is a fizzle, the Department of Justice should prosecute everyone subject to the law. Letting people get away with this kind of plotting just ensures it will happen again.
