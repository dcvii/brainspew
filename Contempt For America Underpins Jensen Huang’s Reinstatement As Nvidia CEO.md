Because I remain addicted to X, I’ve spent a chunk of the past week watching, jaw dropping further by the day, as a group of powerful figures from the tech world have posted over and over and over and over (and over and over) that Jensen Huang should be reinstated as CEO of Nvidia.

Ha! [As if!](https://getyarn.io/yarn-clip/d84fe908-56ac-4c40-ba54-b0a9bd33f6e2)

How pathetic could these guys be? How deluded, how out of touch, how completely disconnected from normal standards of conduct and responsibility, not to mention dignity? Their relentless posting had the feel of an extremely grating and equally quixotic fandom uprising — imagine a million *Star Wars* fans organizing, in unison, to demand redress of the [Han-shot-first injustice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_shot_first) in the form of a mass deletion of all copies of the offending cut of *Star Wars*.

Of course, the campaign to reinstate Huang was much higher-stakes than anything involving Wookiees, but since the thing these guys were calling for would never, ever happen, I found it very easy to ridicule. And the arguments, to the extent there *were* arguments, were quite dumb. *Huang is a great CEO!* I mean, okay? He was a great CEO and then he slept with three people in or around his company, one of them potentially a Chinese spy.

Except maybe Alice Huang *wasn’t* a spy. That was another one of their arguments. The thinking seemed to be that because she might not be a literal spy — I guess it’s technically true that this hasn’t been *proven*, by certain exacting standards, in the [25 days since this story first broke](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html) — that somehow exculpated Huang. And the [Branch Nvidians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_Davidians) (while I wish I’d coame up with that, I stole it from X) didn’t just say it was unproven — they said it had been *debunked*. How had it been debunked? Among other lines of evidence I saw proffered, it had been debunked because Donald Trump had said so. Oh, okay — my bad.

Not to drown us in, like, specifics and facts and reality, but the whole point here is that the United States has a policy limiting China’s access to the powerful Nvidia GPUs that have made the AI revolution possible. Alice Huang was intimately connected to a company, Megaspeed, that seemed to [exist mostly to help China gain access to these chips](https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d), and Jensen Huang was intimately connected to Alice Huang during an ongoing policy dispute over these export controls. It didn’t help matters that he had *also* slept with two of his employees, but no one really cared that much about the sex, and the sex itself would not have undone him. Rather, everyone, including Nvidia’s board, realized he’d done something jarringly irresponsible with regard to U.S. policy and national security, not to mention the future of the company itself. ([This timeline](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/12/business/nvidia-jensen-huang-affairs-timeline.html) has more details, most of them quite damning.)

Huang’s defenders posted so much and made such silly arguments and were acting like such children that I found the whole thing hilarious. I couldn’t look away.

[By now you know who the joke was on:](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/11/23/technology/nvidia-jensen-huang-reinstated) all of us. Well, 99.99999% of us. Huang is back, in large part due to President Trump’s direct intervention. Trump, a normal person, celebrated by inserting Huang into a famous photo of Trump narrowly escaping an assassination attempt, but with a red bra placed in Huang’s hand (it’s a subtle symbol but it represents the ladies he *totally* banged). Also — and I know this sounds too on the nose to be real, but I swear I’m not making it up — the American flag has been replaced with a flag bearing the Nvidia logo.

I’m bothered by this news for all the same reasons as everyone else, but what really jumps out at me here is a subtle but unmistakable contempt for America itself. Those words might sound uncharacteristic coming from me. I think “You hate America!” is the rhetorical equivalent of hurling a urine-filled water balloon at someone. I hate compulsive patriotism. I was politically activated in part by cries of “You hate the troops! You hate America!” leveled at opponents of the Iraq War back in the aughts.

But I think *contempt* — not hate, but *contempt* — is the correct word here. I think it accurately describes how certain people who have immense power at the moment view the United States.

If you look at the dictionary [definitions of](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/contempt) *[contempt](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/contempt)*, you’ll see that a key feature is a lack of respect or reverence. In that manner the concept is distinct from *hate*. You can hate someone while respecting them, but you cannot have contempt for someone while respecting them. Contempt is quieter, more narrowed eyes and icy veins than screaming and explosive rage.

The *[All-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-In_Podcast)* guys led the pro-Huang brigades. On my feed I saw a lot of David Sacks and Jason Calacanis. Like many other people in their orbit, Sacks and Calacanis are generally skeptical of government regulation or intervention that might stymie the ability of innovators to innovate — unless and until they can benefit directly from government action.

No one embodies this more than Sacks, who is reportedly [worth between $250 million and $2 billion](https://fortune.com/2026/03/17/6-billionaires-left-california-billionaire-tax-newsom-brin-page-thiel-spielberg-revenue/). The 2023 Silicon Valley Bank collapse is the most famous example. By rule, the federal government insures deposits up to $250,000 through its Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, but venture capital firms with significantly bigger holdings at SVB were poised to lose a lot more money than that. Sacks and many of his fellow travelers were enraged at the prospect of the federal government not stepping in to help. “Where is \[Jerome\] Powell? Where is \[Janet\] Yellen? Stop this crisis NOW,” he [tweeted as this was all going down](https://twitter.com/DavidSacks/status/1634292056821764099?cxt=HHwWhsDUofD9la4tAAAA). “Announce that all depositors will be safe. Place SVB with a Top 4 bank. Do this before Monday open or there will be contagion and the crisis will spread.”

[The thousands of CEOs and founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/urgent-sign-the-petition-now-thousands-of-startups-and-hundreds-of-thousands-of-startup-jobs-are-at-risk) publicly seeking this bailout got it. The federal government made an exception to the $250,000 rule to cover roughly $17 billion worth of losses (and was prepared to cover a much larger amount). While taxpayer funds were not directly used in this, the government imposed greater fees on some banks to refill its FDIC coffers, and those banks may have [passed those fees on to customers](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/will-americans-end-up-paying-for-bank-failures). “This was not a bailout,” [said Bill Ackman](https://x.com/BillAckman/status/1635109889302315008), a billionaire Trump supporter who is also a part of this crew, [of the bailout](https://www.npr.org/2023/03/13/1163180140/silicon-valley-bank-is-it-a-bailout-barofsky). The hypocrisy coming from this set, from these swashbuckling advocates for entrepreneurship and freedom from the heavy hand of government, was [not remotely subtle](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2023/03/13/silicon-valley-bank-bailout-federal-regulation/). (It should go without saying that under *most* circumstances, these guys [hate socialism](https://www.foxbusiness.com/business-leaders/bill-ackman-sounds-alarm-mamdanis-economic-agenda-socialism-disaster). Other times they make direct recommendations to the government about exactly how it should intervene in the affairs of private businesses.)

To be fair, I don’t know what the right approach is to a bank failure like SVB’s. I do know that the folks who advocated most loudly for the SVB bailout and, more recently, for the Jensen Huang bailout, don’t really seem to have “beliefs” in the traditional sense. With the *All-In* guys in particular, it’s completely impossible to distinguish the opinions they express so promiscuously on social media and their show from their own financial self-interest. They could not be more conflicted or compromised. In most cases it’s *impossible* to know if a given utterance reflects their true belief about some tech policy or business matter or an opportunistic attempt to manipulate the Silicon Valley economy in their favor. I’m not even sure they know!

Sacks, a huge MAGA guy, even got inside the White House, serving as an “AI and crypto czar” for 14 months, putting himself in a remarkable position to [benefit himself and his friends](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/30/technology/david-sacks-white-house-profits.html). The Trump White House waved (and [waived](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Memo-David-Sacks-3.5.2025-1.pdf)) him through — no major ethics issues here. David Sacks had effectively free rein to try to nudge policy on crypto and other tech issues in a David Sacks–friendly direction. Who knows what that looked like! That’s the whole point — we can’t know. It could be as simple as wink-and-nod favors disguised as Sound Policy at the time that’ll be paid back down the line.

When I say that I detect contempt for America in the behavior of Sacks, Calacanis, Ackman, and people like them, what I mean is that they have no reverence for the idea of a country or a people or any institution whose interests might supersede their own. It disgusts them, in fact — who the hell is anyone else to tell them what they can and can’t do?

I think the very concept of “national interest” is a bit alien to this crowd. Not as alien as it is to Trump, who is pathologically incapable of seeing anything but himself in everything he looks at, but a cousin of that viewpoint. The idea of any sort of sacrifice, of sighing and saying “Well, this might not be the best for me, but it *is* for the best” — I don’t think it fully computes for them.

That doesn’t mean that the policy of not selling powerful chips to China is correct, let alone infallibly so. It makes intuitive sense to me, for whatever that’s worth, given the profound dangers posed by this technology and given that China is both a geopolitical rival with expansionist ambitions and a very repressive authoritarian state. The counterarguments I’ve heard also make very little sense. But I’m just some guy and this is outside my usual areas. Who knows?

My point is more that these guys hold in contempt the very idea that there might *be* a policy that runs contrary to their own interests. They hold in contempt the idea of a greater good, of having to sacrifice some of their (billions and billions of!) dollars in the service of a superseding mission or entity. Right now they are pointing to the stock market, which is happy that Huang is back, and hooting and shrieking like baboons. Yes, the market likes Huang. So what?

Alice Huang was clearly part of an effort to circumvent U.S. policy and get more of Nvidia’s advanced chips to China, which at the moment is China’s only real hope of catching up to the U.S. The reporting on this is very solid and more of it is coming out (again, see [the timeline](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/12/business/nvidia-jensen-huang-affairs-timeline.html)). The statements given by Trump and Nvidia are asinine and insulting. Trump [said,](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/23/trump-nvidia-jensen-huang-national-security-01180437) “The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.” Of course it’s impossible that they know that for sure — this scandal broke, like, 30 seconds ago!

Nvidia’s [own statement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director) announcing Huang’s return assured everyone that “in light of recent reporting that Mr. Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort, the Board formed a committee comprised solely of independent directors to investigate, and the committee retained independent counsel to assist it in that investigation. The Board reiterated that the committee’s investigation will proceed to conclusion. Mr. Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it.” That’s big of him! And I’m sure the fact that the guy being investigated has been reinstated will have no impact on the thoroughness or quality of the independent investigation, nor Nvidia’s receptiveness to it.

Again, I don’t think the guys (they’re mostly guys) who caused this outcome *hate* America. That’s not the right word. I think they realize that America has been very good to them, and boy do they love the perks. They’re happy to invoke and to identify with America when they see doing so as aligning with their interests. I mean, look how Jason Calacanis [framed Trump’s efforts yesterday](https://x.com/Jason/status/2124606212868321321): “DADDY’S HOME,” started his post. “The best part of the @realDonaldTrump presidency is his commitment to America winning at all costs, on all fronts and at all times.” Calacanis gleefully explained that “Trump obviously sat the boys down” — by boys he means the China hawks in Trump’s cabinet — “and said, ‘Daddy’s home... now reverse this immediately.’” There’s a certain coherent logic to this if you understand that “America winning” is indistinguishable from “Jason Calacanis winning.”

So they’re happy to invoke unity and patriotism and America when the country serves as a well-stocked ATM for them and their friends. When it doesn’t, that’s when the contempt comes in. That’s when they’re exhausted by the government and sick of all the bureaucracy and corruption. But things always swing back for them eventually, and if they can get *in* on that corruption and bureaucracy, different story: Daddy’s Home.

These guys are celebrating in part because they like Jensen Huang and because his reinstatement will help their own bottom lines. But I think the deeper reason really does go back to contempt: They have won an astonishing, high-profile victory against the most malevolent force they can imagine: anything above themselves.

“President Trump was totally involved and this is a great decision by the NVIDIA board,” White House communications director [Steven Cheung told](https://x.com/rogoswami/status/2124881764535464261) *[Semafor](https://x.com/rogoswami/status/2124881764535464261)* (openly! on the record!). “We have to protect our geniuses.”

Yes, congratulations all around.
