My 81-year-old mother has lived in New York for 30 years, but the city presumes she owes a $55,048 fee for out-of-towners.

Last week, my 81-year-old mother called me with some news: She had been coughing up blood.

Fortunately, it had stopped a couple hours before, she said. My mom seemed flustered, but not panicked. She handles the vagaries of aging with a bemused resignation that healthmaxxing fools like Bryan Johnson would do well to emulate. So she called her doctor and was told that the office would call back with an appointment time.

But the next day, she called me again. This time, she was shaken.

She had received a letter from New York City demanding $55,048 in property taxes on the townhouse in Midtown Manhattan where she has lived since 1996. It was weird, she said, because she didn’t owe $55,048 in property taxes. She asked if I could help her figure out the letter.

The answer turned out to be the city’s new “ [pied-à-terre](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/07/mayor-mamdani-notifies-property-owners-of-new-pied-a-terre-tax) ” tax, the latest make-good on Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s promise to “tax the rich.” Approved by the New York State Legislature in May, the levy is an extra annual property tax of [0.8 percent to 6.5 percent](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/new-york-citys-pied-a-terre-tax/) on high-end apartments and townhouses owned by people whose primary residence is not in New York City.

Except, in its rush to get the loot, the New York City Department of Finance didn’t bother to figure out which properties are really subject to the tax—and which are owned by full-time New Yorkers like my mom, who owns no other properties and now has until August 21 to prove residency or face that $55,048 tax bill. A cursory online search would have revealed the truth, but the city’s bagmen didn’t bother. To add insult to injury, the letter was actually addressed to my father, who died six years ago, in May 2020—in the third-floor bedroom he shared with my mother in the same townhouse.

---

The process speaks to the way Democratic socialists like Mamdani view taxpaying citizens. The city decides what you owe; it is up to you to prove you don’t. To make matters worse, the city’s finance department published the entire list of residences it believes could be subject to the tax, effectively doxxing hundreds of thousands of equity-rich homeowners.

In Mamdani’s New York City, the medium is the message. Not only is he going to tax the rich, he is stoking hatred of his class enemies, including with a [series of “Rental Rip-off” hearings](https://www.thefp.com/p/the-gripes-of-wrath-inside-nycs-rental) soliciting grievances against landlords, and in a [video filmed in April](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FLKZnVB4F9k) outside the Manhattan penthouse of billionaire Ken Griffin, CEO of investment firm Citadel, where Mamdani promised to collect pied-à-terre taxes on the $238 million property. Last week, true to form, the mayor issued a cheeky [social media missive](https://x.com/NYCMayor/status/2080381372510724286) for pied-à-terre owners: “Check your mailbox when you’re back in the five boroughs—because you’ve got mail.” The new tax, he warned, “is coming soon.”

Despite this predictably sloppy rollout, I am of two minds about the pied-à-terre tax.

New York City’s government is funded by some of the highest state and local tax rates in the country. Its budget is larger than the entire state of Florida, which now has close to 24 million people, compared to the city’s 8.6 million. Our school system now spends over $50,000 per student annually.

On the other hand, New York voters, in their infinite wisdom, [last year elected a socialist mayor](https://urldefense.com/v3/__https://alexberenson.substack.com/p/zohran-mamdani-will-freeze-the-rent__;!!CxwJSw!LUAqTvNrg9jUQdFwjp0knbqa0Odrl2u7-CIcORnhnoE6wteZhzOuiaERu1wQ0a8x7fmatfRBYB7N9ZyHI1-KUho$) who never met a taxpayer-financed giveaway he couldn’t expand. And with a projected budget gap of $8.8 billion in 2028, the city’s inexhaustible demand for cash is about to get even more inexhaustible.

Of all the ways that New York City could raise funds, increasing taxes on expensive second homes that are essentially capital vaults for foreign investors—and some wealthy Americans—may be the best. Yes, these folks do not cost the city much. They don’t live here, so they don’t consume many services, after all. But they also don’t contribute anything, and their empty townhouses and apartments lead to a hollowed-out feeling at street level in parts of Manhattan.

Furthermore, despite the concerns of the real-estate industry, an extra annual tax will probably make little difference to the owners. The maintenance costs on these trophy properties are insane already. For a visiting billionaire, renting a hotel suite for a few days would be a far better value, if value were the point.

So if Mamdani is going to reach into anyone’s pockets, and he surely is, he might as well start with Chinese billionaires.

But my mom, who is not a Chinese billionaire, has no idea how to get an exemption—the letter she received does not explain exactly what proof is needed. Instead, it directs anyone wrongly hit with the surcharge to a city website where “you will be asked to upload documents to support your response.” Just getting to the document page requires an “NYC.ID” account and the ability to use the city’s borough-block-lot search engine, which are nontrivial tasks for an 81-year-old.

---

Still, I’m confident that, with my help, my mom will get this sorted out before August 21, the deadline the city has set.

In the end, the most telling part of this whole mess was the response on social media. When I posted about what had happened on X, it went viral, racking up almost five million views and a great deal of blowback. [One user](https://x.com/bri_guy_ny/status/2081878867011109006?s=20), whose X biography claims he is a “dad, lawyer, progressive, and former 2020 Bernie delegate,” wrote: “My Mommy owns a townhouse that’s worth upwards of $8 million dollars, and the government is making me FILL OUT A FORM. I am deeply oppressed. Boo hoo for me.*”*

Yes, my mother is lucky. The townhouse has been a great investment. It is her primary asset—which is why the thought that she might be stuck with a crushing tax bill upset her more than literal bloodletting. But it’s not just an asset. She loves living there. She and my dad believed in New York. They backed their belief with cash and committed to the city. They held on through September 11, through the financial crisis, through Covid—and they never purchased a second home.

Of course, Mamdani’s pied-à-terre tax isn’t just about pied-à-terres, or even the billionaires who own them. It’s about stoking resentment toward an ever-expanding list of class enemies. As it turns out, “tax the rich” has no fixed definition of rich, and the line only moves in one direction.
