There is no shortage of genuinely extraordinary black intellectuals. The problem was that many of them refuse to play the ideological role liberal elites wanted them to play.

The Jason Arday scandal has left the academic world reeling. Found dead in his family’s apartment last Friday, Arday has been the subject of intense scrutiny over the past month. Appointed in 2023 as Cambridge University’s youngest-ever black professor, he faced extensive and credible allegations of plagiarism that exploded into public view last month. Those allegations led to public interest in the (literally) incredible details of his life. Arday claimed to have run 600 miles in six days—which would make him one of the best ultra-marathon runners on Earth. He claimed that he couldn’t speak until the age of 11 and couldn’t read until 18, but somehow managed to complete a PhD around 30. And he claimed to have raised the equivalent of several million U.S. dollars for charity.

Jason Arday was either the most interesting man in the world, or he was a liar. It turned out to be the latter.

All this has revealed many unflattering things about academia: DEI invites fraud by lowering standards, the bigotry of low expectations is alive and well, and a disturbing number of “experts”—not just Arday but all the Cambridge professors and BBC journalists who co-signed him—are compromised by ideology, cowardice, or both.

But the Arday scandal has also revealed something else: White liberals in the English-speaking world want to believe in the archetype of the up-from-nothing black intellectual who, despite huge disadvantages, goes on to achieve greatness. In fact, they want to believe in this sort of story so badly that they are willing to turn off every ounce of skepticism that they would normally bring to bear on fantastical claims.

---

The irony, however, is that we already have *many* examples of up-from-nothing black intellectuals who go on to greatness. It’s just that they usually turn out to be conservatives—or if not conservatives, then at least out-of-step with the left-wing religion of racial justice.

Consider [Thomas Sowell](https://www.city-journal.org/article/the-nonconformist). Sowell was born in the Jim Crow South and grew up in a house without hot water or electricity. His father died before he was born, and his mother died soon after. As a teenager, he did a brief stint in a homeless shelter, sleeping with a knife under his pillow. Years later, he somehow managed to make it to Harvard University—before affirmative action existed to cast a doubt over such achievements—and he began what would become one of the great intellectual careers in modern American history.

Or think of [Roland Fryer](https://youtu.be/m8xWOlk3WIw?si=tsYmsq9PVAipPFmF). Fryer was abandoned at birth by his mother. His father, meanwhile, was an abusive, gambling alcoholic who ended up in prison for rape. He also spent quite a bit of time with his great-aunt and cousins, who ran a major crack cocaine operation. Perhaps unsurprisingly, by his teenage years, Fryer was involved in petty crime. Yet he managed to get into college on an athletic scholarship and then caught the economics bug. He would go on to become an award-winning economics professor at Harvard.

Or consider Glenn Loury. Loury grew up in a redlined, working-class neighborhood on the South Side of Chicago in the 1950s. During his teens, he fathered two children, and supported them by working in a printing plant—all while taking classes at a community college. Eventually, he worked his way up to MIT, where he earned a PhD. And despite his later crack cocaine addiction, Loury went on to an illustrious career as an economist and political commentator.

They are serving their intended purpose. Black intellectuals who don’t do this are not just useless; they are an active threat to the white guilt complex.

In an alternate reality, Sowell, Fryer, and Loury would be heroes of the left, just like Jason Arday was until his downfall. After all, it is the left and not the right that frequently talks about “centering black voices.” But there’s one problem. Though progressives would never say this out loud, people like Sowell, Fryer, and Loury don’t serve the purpose for which black intellectuals are intended, which is not to advance research or fight for the interests of black people but rather to scratch a very specific psychological itch in the white liberal psyche.

In the progressive mind, white people are forever the problem and need to be told that purging the racism within their hearts is the key to black advancement. Black intellectuals who ritualistically affirm this belief—people like Ta-Nehisi Coates, Ibram X. Kendi, and Nikole Hannah-Jones—are useful. They are serving their intended purpose. Black intellectuals who don’t do this are not just useless; they are an active threat to the white guilt complex.

Sowell, for example, has spent his career arguing, quite persuasively, that discrimination is not the only possible cause of racial disparities and that culture matters a great deal, too. Fryer has argued, again persuasively, that American police departments are [no likelier](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22399/w22399.pdf) to use deadly force on black suspects than on suspects who aren’t black; that Justice Department pattern-or-practice investigations into police agencies (often favored by Democratic administrations) [cause an increase](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27324/w27324.pdf) in violent crime, which hurts the black community most; and that the “ [acting white](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11334) ” penalty imposed by the black community on its own members is a real thing. Loury, for his part, has a [long-running podcast](https://open.spotify.com/show/1242saBs2GjgmEqsRQwnks?si=753e4da4ff5049c3) with John McWhorter (a self-described “cranky liberal Democrat”) that has featured some of the best criticisms of woke racial politics out there.

So with all of the genuinely inspiring black intellectuals rendered useless by their stubborn independence of mind, who is left to scratch this guilty itch in the liberal psyche? Well, it would seem that the second string has had to suffice. And that’s where the Jason Ardays of the world come in. It should go without saying that Arday’s death is a terrible tragedy that no one should celebrate. At the same time, it is true to say that this tragedy was enabled by the psychological need among liberal elites for a black avatar of their guilt.

Arday’s death should be a call for liberals and progressives to look inward—not to expunge the supposed demon of white supremacy, but to look at the nature of white guilt itself. Ultimately, white guilt is a virus that leads its victims to prioritize their own need for catharsis over the public’s need for truth. The moment that elites in the West have this much-needed epiphany en massewill be the moment that we ensure the tragic case of Jason Arday never repeats itself.
