For more than a century, feminism fought to expand women’s choices, and in that time a lot of progress was made.

The right to vote. The right to work. Equal access to education. Protection from discrimination because you’re pregnant.

Those battles changed America. But, somewhere along the way, a movement built around expanding women’s freedom became a source of profound disagreement over what women should actually do with that freedom. And increasingly, women find themselves judged from both directions for making the “wrong” choices.

There’s the trope of the work-obsessed, childless “cat lady,” the association of feminism with woke politics and cancel culture, and the notion that feminists want to advance women over men. The stereotype on the other side is just as blunt: that anti-feminists want to send women back to the 1940s, take away their agency, and “keep women in the kitchen.”

All of this raises the question: Do we need feminism more than ever? Or... not at all? And if we do need it, what is its purpose in 2026?

To discuss these critically important topics, we invited two women from different sides of the debate to Nashville. Author and podcast host Allie Beth Stuckey took on political organizer Deja Foxx for our latest Things That Matter debate, hosted by Kelly O’Grady.
