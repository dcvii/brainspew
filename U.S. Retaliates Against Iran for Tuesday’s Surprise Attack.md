American forces carried out strikes against [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) on Wednesday night, in what the U.S. military called a “powerful response” to Iran’s missile attacks on U.S. forces in Jordan.

The strikes came hours after President [Trump](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/donald-trump) vowed to retaliate for the [surprise ballistic missile strike](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-gambles-on-escalation-with-surprise-attack-on-u-s-forces-72cfd422?mod=article_inline), dashing hopes for an imminent diplomatic breakthrough. Though the assault was larger in scope than recent U.S. attacks, it didn’t amount to a return to major combat operations, according to a U.S. official.

U.S. forces carried out a “heavy wave” of strikes, attacking dozens of targets, including military command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defense sites, and maritime capabilities, said U.S. Central Command after the strikes were completed. “The strikes aimed to further diminish threats posed by Iran and its proxies to American forces, commercial shipping, and neighboring Gulf countries,” the command said.

“We are going to be hitting them very hard,” Trump said in the Oval Office on Wednesday, “because it’s our turn.”

Iran’s unprovoked attack on U.S. troops, which took place late Tuesday Eastern time, shattered a brief ceasefire, after both sides paused strikes on Friday in order to [give negotiators a chance](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-and-oman-seek-agreement-on-the-hormuz-in-bid-to-jumpstart-peace-talks-d4c442ac?mod=article_inline) to hammer out an arrangement for ships to pass through the Strait of Hormuz. The Trump team had hoped those talks, which [collapsed on Tuesday](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-rejects-omani-proposal-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-demands-more-control-20ec9e92?mod=article_inline), would cool tempers and juice negotiations for a broader agreement. Iran carried out its attacks on Jordan hours after the end of the discussions.

Trump told reporters on Wednesday that the Iranians had been pleading with him not to retaliate in the hours after the attack, “but they nevertheless took a shot” and now “know it is coming.” A counterstrike was inevitable, he said: “Will I always hit back? Pretty much.”

While Tuesday’s Iranian missile attack was successfully intercepted, the barrage intensified pressure on the president to respond and threatened to once again restart hostilities as the war with Iran enters its sixth month.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu met with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth in Washington on Wednesday as the Trump administration weighed its next move.

In a White House meeting on Tuesday, Trump and Netanyahu did what a senior Israeli official called a clinical review of three strategies toward Iran: a diplomatic agreement, intensifying economic pressure and ramping up the blockade, or renewing military action. U.S. and Israeli officials weighed costs and timelines for each.

Netanyahu didn’t express a preference for any of the options and told Trump it was his decision, the Israeli official said. He told Trump that diplomacy could be pursued alongside greater economic pressure such as restricting Iran’s overland trade routes while negotiations continue.

Hours after the Iranian strikes on Jordan, the U.S. military said that American and Saudi aircraft launched strikes against Iran-backed groups in Iraq.
