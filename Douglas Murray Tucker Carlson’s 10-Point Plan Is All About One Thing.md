He’s supposedly pitching a vision for America. But he can’t help but talk about Israel instead.

Tucker Carlson has a podcast. But it increasingly seems as though he doesn’t just want you to watch it. He wants you to vote for it.

Why else would he have spent recent weeks suggesting that America needs some kind of third party to save itself? The founding members, seemingly, would be an *Avengers* -style team [consisting of](https://x.com/FmrRepMTG/status/2083568963846303896) Tucker, former representatives Marjorie Taylor Greene and Thomas Massie, former counterterrorism director Joe Kent, and some of their spouses. To show he means business, he devoted an interminable (technically, [98-minute](https://youtu.be/u5h2OCcKYkc?si=mMnFWXrWztoYuCU9)) episode this week to his 10-point plan to save America.

Some people have suggested that Carlson is clearly lining up to run for political office. Others who have known him for years point out that Carlson has neither the discipline nor the patience required for a political run. We now have some fresh evidence of that: He seems to lack the patience to write down 10 points without boring himself.

The Carlson plan to save America includes the following list of aims he believes our country must pursue: fairness, sovereignty, productivity, beauty, health, honesty, optimism, wisdom, decency, and unity. Like many lists created by Carlson’s erstwhile opponents on the socialist left, these are slogans that are meant to be unopposable. Or to put it another way, as a logician taught me many years ago, there is no point in uttering a phrase the opposite of which would be uttered only by a madman.

---

For instance, it is a good rule of thumb—though one followed by too few politicians—that you should never use a phrase like “I believe children are the future,” and not only because it is a cliché. It’s also because you won’t soon hear anyone say that children are the past.

Tucker Carlson never got this memo. Nobody needed him to put forward a list of virtues that pretty much everybody affirms.

The giveaway, however, is that Carlson does not even have the discipline to flesh out any of these points without collapsing into vagueness and dark insinuation. In one moment he calls for fairness, an important value for the right to consider, especially in contrast with the left’s pursuit of “equity.” But Carlson does not have the temper for such an exploration. No sooner does he hail the importance of fairness than he is off once railing about the “Epstein class.” The supposed connection to fairness is that Jeffrey Epstein was “possibly a kid-toucher, clearly involved with Mossad.” So when Carlson invokes fairness, an unimpeachable value, he’s really urging greater suspicion of Israel.

With some degree of hope one might go onto the next virtue. What does Carlson have to say about the importance of “sovereignty”? Once again, there seems to be a certain rut that he cannot help falling in. National sovereignty is important because the opposite of it is apparently what we have right now—specifically, the U.S. being “under bribery or threat or both by Israel.” Something which apparently “everybody knows.”

Carlson is not really interested in fleshing out his thoughts on sovereignty or wisdom. That is because where he once had thoughts, he now only has spasms.

Right around now, some of the cannier readers might have noticed a certain *idée fixe* in this list. When it comes to “decency,” instead of the many things that could be said about the erosion of America’s cultural mores, Carlson informs us that “Israel’s not the only country that’s committed genocide and neither is Nazi Germany. It’s happened a lot.” Apparently the way to be decent is not to commit genocide. Unlike those darn Jews and—I suppose we must concede—the Nazis.

Carlson does manage to keep his mind off of Israel for a few fleeting moments. He stays long enough on the question of health to make the insight that it includes not only being physically healthy “but also spiritually healthy, clear-eyed, and bright.” For someone who claims to have been attacked by a demon in his bed, a demon that left strange, doglike claw marks on his body that were definitely not caused by the Carlson family’s dogs, this is quite an interesting pitch. Hearing from Carlson that we should be spiritually healthy is like being offered a lesson in weight loss by Kim Jong Un.

There was a time when Carlson might well have been able to come up with a real list of conservative virtues. But after the self-punishment of his past few years, he can barely begin down that track without derailing into the ravine of conspiracism. Carlson is not really interested in fleshing out his thoughts on sovereignty or wisdom. That is because where he once had thoughts, he now only has spasms. And those spasms relate to a tedious set of obsessions that always come back to Jews, Israel, Epstein, and very little else.

Have no doubt that there are people shouting in Carlson’s ear that this is what the masses want. The reality is that this is what a small number of online obsessives want. They want someone who can give them an itch and then volunteer to scratch it for them, over and over again. That may be a good model for the podcasting world. But as Carlson’s failure to extrapolate his own ideas demonstrates, it’s a wretched way of preparing for government.
