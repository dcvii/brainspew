I have a first cousin who constantly posts on Facebook. And that is a great thing. She is a clear extrovert eager to share the details of her life with the world. I am happy for her. What bothers me more and more are the constant images of sodium-laden, fatty foods. We’re talking subway sandwiches from Subway, a dinner of salted ham, macaroni and cheese, and sodium bomb southern biscuits. She wants us to be there as she savors tasty food, food that is bad for her blood pressure. I am the last person to cast aspersions on my cousin. But cast aspersions I will because I love my cousin. My faithful readers know my weaknesses for chocolate cake over a lifetime. I was killing myself with gluttony and lousy blood pressure readings. See *[Chocolate Cake](https://twyman.substack.com/p/chocolate-cake?utm_source=publication-search).*

Bad choices can feel so good in the moment. It is a cultural thing if I might be so bold.

Some scholars, intellectuals and writers argue that systemic racism determines cardiovascular health. High blood pressure flows from evil systemic racism. See **“Racism and Hypertension: A Review of the Empirical Evidence and Implications for Clinical Practice” by** *Authors:* Elizabeth Brondolo et al. (*American Journal of Hypertension*, 2011), **“Perceived Racial Discrimination and Hypertension: A Comprehensive Systematic Review”** *Authors:* C. M. Dolezsar et al. (*BMC Public Health*, 2014) and **“Lifelong Discrimination Linked to High Blood Pressure in Black People” / CARDIA Study Findings** *Authors:* Allana T. Forde et al. (*Journal of the American Heart Association*, 2021).

*“I thought you had the biscuit after all of that bellyaching.”- my spouse, ten seconds ago*

*\*  
*If I use myself as a case study, I must disagree with these arguments linking systemic racism to high blood pressure. I have suffered from increasingly high blood pressure and yet I don’t experience systemic racism. In over 1,100 essays since March 23, 2023, have I written about systemic racism weighing me down in life? Have I lamented lifelong discrimination as a black man in America? No, and to the contrary, I have been ceaselessly optimistic and upbeat about my American life. There is no systemic racism for me in San Diego in the year of our Lord 2026.

So, if this is true, why did I suffer from blood pressure readings in recent months like 143/84, 144/91 and 151/84? It wasn’t evil frameworks and diabolical systems. It was because of my daily stress *and* my choice to eat chocolate cake nearly every day and candy bars and salty restaurant food and bags of salty chips. I am being honest and self-aware. It was not villainous white supremacy and white privilege that pushed my arteries to the breaking point. It was me, my impulsive cravings and lack of discipline.

We really let black Americans like my cousin and others off the hook when the credentialed attribute hypertension to abstract impersonal forces rather than biscuits, fried chicken and chocolate cake. There, I said it. I am not a doctor but I can share with you what made a radical difference in my life. I listened to my doctor. I listened to my wife and an unnamed close friend. I stopped devouring chocolate cake. I started to have broccoli for breakfast, peanuts and dark chocolate and blueberries for lunch. I listened to my great doctor and lived a life of compliance. I took the prescribed Magnesium Glycinate in the morning with my new prescription for Amlodipine Besylate. I dined on steel-cut oats, honey, dark chocolate, bananas and blueberries in the mornings. I continued to walk 10,000 steps at least three days a week, although I should do more.

And you know something?

My blood pressure life changed. I began to see green lines on my daily blood pressure readings. I even dropped down to 98/69 on September 9, 2026. This was the best recorded blood pressure reading in my life! It was followed by civilized readings unknown to my arteries—120/75, 102/70, 120/70, 118/76, 111/66.

Was ***systemic racism*** magically erased from my world over the past month or two? No. What changed was I grew up and said “NO” to daily inhalation of chocolate cake. I ate better and was intentional about what I consumed. I didn’t mindlessly go to 7-11 in the afternoon for chocolate cake anymore. I took my meds. And I walked 10,000 steps a day and took my meds some more.

Suppose I had fallen victim to the dogma that ***systemic racism*** was to blame for my high blood pressure readings in the morning and at night? I would be like my cousin advertising my love of food elevating my blood pressure and blaming THE MAN, er, I mean ***systemic racism*** for my elevated blood pressure readings like 157/95 on August 16 in the morning. That is stroke level, ladies and gentlemen.

**Conclusion:** Like many black Americans, my cousin has a problem. She throws all caution to the wind when it comes to her diet. The salt and sodium expands and expands her blood vessels. My fear is some woke doctor will take one look at my cousin’s presumably high blood pressure and blame evil ***systemic racism***. It is a falsehood and my cousin will be let off the hook.

The aforementioned authors will say her blood pressure is the price my cousin pays for living as a Black woman in America. Lifelong discrimination causes high blood pressure. Such a lie and deception worthy of an AI model. Put down the sub from Subway, cousin, and try broccoli, blueberries and bananas.

I gave up chocolate cake and my blood vessels thank me.
