As longstanding and attentive readers (H/T Jack) are aware, I started a habit of long walks whilst on my April/May sabbatical in Crete, and I have continued ever since. I average about three a week, and they are a brisk 6.72 miles (or at least that’s what the software that tracks me says they are). Just over two hours in length, the podcast “The Rest Is History” is my companion, unless a new “A History of the Americans” has dropped, in which case it takes precedence.

Focused as I am on learning more about history, I find that sometimes, my mind wanders. This essay is about some of those wanderings from my last walk

### The Golf Trip

In mid-October, I will fly to South Carolina and drive to Kiawah Island to meet up with a stout group of UVA Sigma Chi’s, vintage 1987/1988, there to bunk at one of the guys’ places and play three rounds of golf as two foursomes at three different courses in the area. Although I graduated in 1987, I didn’t pledge until my second year, so the 1988 guys were in my pledge class. I was a late addition, as one of the other guys had to drop for family reasons.

I have spent my entire adult life silently judging men who drag golf clubs through airports. I simply couldn’t imagine why I would want to lug all that weight around, as my urge to golf declined precipitously after college. But here I am, recently having purchased a piece of luggage form fit for the purpose of moving golf clubs by air, so that I can now be one of those guys.

Truth is, my Dad was a big golf/traveler, and he would take a long golf vacations “with the boys” each year to Florida. As my father’s life has been my guide to how to do things (especially in how to shuck and jive with other men), when this opportunity presented itself, I decided to get over my ridiculous judgment and join in.

The coordination for this trip has been done by text message, and the running log is a tribute to how funny these guys are. I’ve always said that joining Sigma Chi was the best decision I made in college (first in the context of having a group of great guys to help me over the incineration of my tender heart committed by a lovely Texas debutante, then simply for the presence of genuinely great guys), and this group of eight was very close. I am very much looking forward to the trip and the chicanery that follows.

Following the talk of betting/tournaments/etc., one of the guys posted a screenshot of his handicap, encouraging the others to “show theirs” which most did. Having only recently re-established one with the minimum of three rounds required, it appears that I am sixteen strokes worse that anyone else at this game. Some of these guys may remember that I was substantially better than that in college, so there are baseless accusations flying that I am a “sandbagger”. What they do not account for is thirty-nine years of one or two rounds a year and a swing that can use some serious work.

And it is serious work that I’ve been doing. The internet is a gold mine of golf instruction, and I have been heading over to the practice range every day hitting balls, chipping, and putting. Things are coming back slowly. My 21-year-old performance is not going to be challenged, but I am prepared to admit that I may play to something south of the expanded handicap I am playing with, therein reinforcing the “sandbagger” charge.

The fact that I am out of practice is my own fault. I had a membership in a golf course continuously since 1992, but I shudder to think what the cost per round based on annual dues has been.

### The Reading List

I recently addled-on here about taking on the Top 500 Novels of all time, something I do plan on as soon as I am finished the books I’m reading now (re-reading “The Lord of the Rings”, reading a book by an attendant of the Japanese Empress in the 11th Century called “The Pillow Book”, and “The Creation of the American Republic” by Gordon Wood), but on my walks, I’ve been thinking about how to approach the 47 books on the list I’ve already read. The first (“Don Quixote”), is one I’ve read before, and on my walks, I find myself wondering whether a task of this magnitude should start with “skipping” a book. Seems a little half-assed, no? But if one simply wants to work through 500 books in one’s allotted time, time really does matter.

I think I’m going to follow these rules:

- I do not have to read any book I have already read, unless I want to.
- Any book on the list that I have tried to read and put down out of disgust at its being unreadable gibberish (I see you, “Moby Dick”), I do not have to read.
- Any book I start I will try to finish, but I will not be obsessed.
- Any book that I have for whatever reason, come to believe would be a waste of time to read, I will not read.

So, I have a feeling I’ll be reading somewhat less than 500 books by the time I’m done (or dead), but 350-400 seem doable.

### Things I Don't Care For While Walking

- I don't care for dogs being wheeled about in baby prams. This is just silly.
- I don't care for electric bicycles. People on them invariably could use the calorie burn of a manual bike, and they drive too fast on narrow trails.
- I don't care for people who do not exchange greetings. Rare in this country, but it happens.
- I don't care for over-cologned teenage boys using the trail for their walk to school. Hai-Karate in the morning is bracing.

### Things I Enjoy While Walking

- Non-malodorous teenagers using the trail for their walk to school in the morning. Chirping away with each other as they walk.
- Squirrels. Rabbits.
- Commuters. There is a solid group of folks commuting each day headed mostly to the various tendrils of our health care industry located just off the trail. I like that some folks choose to walk to work.
- Large green round pods, could be chestnuts or walnuts (I'm not an arborist), suitable for throwing at trees while on the move as I channel my inner Dothraki screamer.
- Available restrooms.
- The black cat who positions him/herself along the trail proximate to his/her dwelling.
- Conjuring Substack content.

### Walking Alone

I see groups of two or three on the trail, and I think about whether that is for me. I think not. Not for these two-hour jobs at least. I really like slipping deeply into podcast mode, and I wouldn’t want to have to hold up my end of witty, engaging conversation for that long. The Kitten and I walk together sometimes in the local nature preserve, and we do quite well, largely because of her strength as an interlocutor AND the fact that they last about an hour. The other thing is that the trail is mostly one person comfortably wide, and so I generally follow behind which means it is hard to hear anyway.

I have begun to wonder on my walks, every one of which has been conducted in temperate conditions, whether I will be as hearty a stroller when the cold arrives. I think I will, but I really have not experience to draw on. It seems pretty straightforward, no? Bundle up, hat, gloves, etc. I imagine things can get a little sporty on the face, but I can’t imagine investing in a balaclava. And, it really doesn’t get THAT cold here in the People’s State of Maryland. Maybe once in a while.
