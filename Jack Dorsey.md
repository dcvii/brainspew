**Jack Patrick Dorsey** (born November 19, 1976) [^3] is an American tech entrepreneur and businessman who co-founded [Twitter, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter,_Inc. "Twitter, Inc."), which operated social media service [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"); financial technology company [Block, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block,_Inc. "Block, Inc."), which owns and operates [Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_\(financial_services\) "Square (financial services)"), [Cash App](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_App "Cash App"), [Afterpay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterpay "Afterpay"), [Bitkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitkey "Bitkey"), and [Tidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_\(service\) "Tidal (service)"); and microblogging social networking service [Bluesky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluesky "Bluesky").

As of February 2026, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* estimated his net worth to be $5.9 billion.[^4]

## Early life

Dorsey was born and raised in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), Missouri.[^5] [^6] His father is Timothy G. Dorsey and his mother is Marcia A. Dorsey ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Smith).[^7] [^8] Jack Dorsey is partly of [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians "Italians") descent on his mother's side.[^9] His father worked for a company that developed [mass spectrometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometer "Mass spectrometer") and his mother was a homemaker.[^10] He was raised [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church").[^11]

Dorsey attended [Bishop DuBourg High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_DuBourg_High_School "Bishop DuBourg High School").[^11] In his younger days, he worked occasionally as a fashion model.[^12] By age 14, he had become interested in [dispatch routing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispatch_\(logistics\) "Dispatch (logistics)"). Dorsey enrolled at the [University of Missouri–Rolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_University_of_Science_and_Technology "Missouri University of Science and Technology") in 1995 and attended for two-plus years [^11] before transferring to [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") in 1997, but he dropped out two years later, one semester short of graduating.[^13] He came up with the idea that eventually became [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") while studying at New York University.[^14]

While working on dispatching as a programmer, Dorsey moved to [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California").[^15] [^16] In 2000, Dorsey started his company in [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California "Oakland, California") to dispatch couriers, taxis, and emergency services from the Web.[^17] His other projects and ideas at this time included networks of medical devices and a "frictionless service market".[^17] In July 2000, building on dispatching [^18] and inspired in part by [LiveJournal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LiveJournal "LiveJournal") and by [AOL Instant Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM_\(software\) "AIM (software)"), he had the idea for a Web-based realtime status/short message communication service.[^17]

When he first saw implementations of [instant messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_messaging "Instant messaging"), Dorsey wondered whether the software's user status output could be shared easily among friends.[^18] He approached [Odeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeo "Odeo"), which at the time happened to be interested in [text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging "Text messaging").[^18] Dorsey and [Biz Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biz_Stone "Biz Stone") decided that SMS text suited the status-message idea, and built a prototype of Twitter in about two weeks.[^18] The idea attracted many users at Odeo and investment from [Evan Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Williams_\(Internet_entrepreneur\) "Evan Williams (Internet entrepreneur)"),[^18] a co-founder of that firm in 2005 who had left [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") after selling [Pyra Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyra_Labs "Pyra Labs") and [Blogger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blogger_\(service\) "Blogger (service)").

## Career

### Twitter

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Jack_Dorsey-20080723.jpg/250px-Jack_Dorsey-20080723.jpg)

Dorsey in 2008

[Noah Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Glass "Noah Glass"), [Evan Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Williams_\(Internet_entrepreneur\) "Evan Williams (Internet entrepreneur)"), and [Biz Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biz_Stone "Biz Stone") co-founded Odeo, later renamed Obvious Corporation, which then spun off Twitter, Inc. Dorsey became Twitter's [chief executive officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") (CEO) when it was founded in 2007.[^18] [^19] [^20] As CEO, Dorsey saw the startup through two rounds of funding by venture capitalists.[^20] As the service began to grow in popularity, Dorsey chose the improvement of uptime as top priority,[^21] even over creating revenue—which, as of 2008, Twitter was not designed to earn.[^22] Dorsey described the commercial use of Twitter and its API as two things that could lead to paid features.[^22] His three guiding principles, which he says the company shares, are simplicity, constraint, and craftsmanship.[^22]

Dorsey lost his position as CEO in 2008, reportedly for leaving work early to enjoy other pursuits, such as [yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga "Yoga") and [fashion design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design "Fashion design").[^23] On October 16, 2008,[^24] Williams took over as CEO, while Dorsey became chairman of the board.[^25] [^26] During his time as chairman, Dorsey joined several State Department delegations, including a trip to Iraq in April 2009, led by [Jared Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Cohen "Jared Cohen").[^27] [^28] [^29] In November, when Iranians took to the streets in the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_presidential_election_protests "2009 Iranian presidential election protests"), Twitter was scheduled to conduct maintenance of its site, which would entail temporarily shutting down Twitter's servers. Dorsey responded to a request from Cohen to delay the maintenance so that it would not affect the protests in Iran, because Iranians were using Twitter to communicate and coordinate.[^30] Since President Obama had announced that there would be no meddling in Iran, the move sparked controversy.[^31] [^32] [^33] In February 2010, Dorsey was part of another State Department delegation, this time to Russia.[^34] [^35] In March 2011, he returned to Twitter as executive chairman after [Dick Costolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Costolo "Dick Costolo") replaced Williams as CEO.[^36] In July 2015, Costolo resigned as CEO, with Dorsey assuming the post of interim CEO.[^37] He was named permanent CEO in October 2015.[^38]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Obama_Tweeting.jpg/250px-Obama_Tweeting.jpg)

Dorsey and President Barack Obama at Twitter Town Hall in July 2011

In May 2016, Dorsey announced that Twitter would not count photos and links in the 140-character limit to free up more space for text. This was an attempt to entice new users, since the number of tweets per day had dropped from about 500 million in September 2013 and its peak of 661 million in August 2014 to about 300 million in January 2016.[^39]

On November 22, 2016, Dorsey was briefly suspended from his own Twitter account with 3.9 million followers. After restoring the account, Dorsey tweeted that the suspension was due to an "internal mistake".[^40]

Twitter introduced recommendation algorithms in the main feed in 2016.[^41] Dorsey wrote in 2022 that "The biggest mistake I made was continuing to invest in building tools for us to manage the public conversation, versus building tools for the people using Twitter to easily manage it for themselves." [^42]

In February 2017, Dorsey and Executive Chairman [Omid Kordestani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omid_Kordestani "Omid Kordestani") matched a $530,000 donation to the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") (ACLU) raised by Twitter staffers. Their match brought the total donation to $1.59 million.[^43]

In March 2018, Dorsey announced that an improved version of the [verification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_verification_service "Identity verification service") would be coming to Twitter. The purpose of redesigning verification was to let people verify more facts about themselves, emphasizing proof of identity.[^44] The overhaul was not in place before the [U.S midterm election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_midterm_election "United States midterm election") of [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_elections "2018 United States elections") to help in [verifying the identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentication "Authentication") of the candidates.[^45]

In September 2018, Dorsey testified before the [Senate Intelligence Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence "United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence") alongside [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") [COO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer "Chief operating officer") [Sheryl Sandberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Sandberg "Sheryl Sandberg") about the use of social media by Russia and others to meddle in the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election"). Following this testimony, Twitter shares fell six percent.[^46]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/President_Trump_Meets_with_Twitter_CEO_Jack_Dorsey_%2840715975903%29.jpg/250px-President_Trump_Meets_with_Twitter_CEO_Jack_Dorsey_%2840715975903%29.jpg)

President Donald Trump with Dorsey in the Oval Office of the White House on April 23, 2019

Dorsey met privately with U.S. President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") at the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") and discussed Trump's concerns that Twitter had [limited or removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_banning "Shadow banning") some of his Twitter followers, and those of conservatives. After the meeting, Dorsey tweeted that their discussion included making Twitter "healthier and more civil".[^47] A week earlier, Dorsey took part in a [TED talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_\(conference\) "TED (conference)") about the social media platform's spread of abuse and misinformation, which has brought him criticism.[^48]

On August 30, 2019, Dorsey's personal Twitter account was allegedly breached for nearly an hour by a group calling itself the Chuckling Squad, posting and retweeting numerous racist tweets.[^49] [^50]

On October 23, 2019, Twitter's stock price fell by nearly 21 percent, to $30.75.[^51] The reason was an earnings miss off a third quarter report, which Twitter blamed on ad targeting problems. Dorsey had been making a concerted effort to dampen the effect that Twitter had on political elections, which entailed banning all political ads. This was also seen as a large contributor to the drop. Dorsey announced that, as of November 22, 2019, Twitter would ban all political advertising, which applied globally to all marketing campaigns about political issues.[^52] This policy was removed in August 2023 after ownership was transferred to Elon Musk.[^53]

On February 29, 2020, it was announced [^54] that activist hedge fund [Elliott Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Management_Corporation "Elliott Management Corporation") led by billionaire [Paul Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Singer_\(businessman\) "Paul Singer (businessman)") was looking to oust Dorsey and nominate four directors to Twitter's board, including Elliott's senior portfolio manager Jesse Cohn.[^55] The two parties reached an agreement days later, with Dorsey remaining CEO.[^56] [^57]

In October 2020, Dorsey was one of several tech firm CEOs subpoenaed by the [US Senate Commerce Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Commerce,_Science,_and_Transportation "United States Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation"). [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_\(United_States\) "Republican Party (United States)") [Roger Wicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Wicker "Roger Wicker"), who chaired the committee, led the charge to force the CEOs of Twitter, [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") and [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") to testify about the legal immunity the tech platforms receive under [Section 230 of the Communications Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_230 "Section 230") of 1996.[^58]

He announced his immediate resignation as Twitter's CEO on November 29, 2021. Dorsey was replaced by the company's former CTO [Parag Agrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parag_Agrawal "Parag Agrawal"), who took over as CEO. Dorsey continued to lead as the CEO of [Block, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block,_Inc. "Block, Inc.")[^59] [^60] [^61] In May 2022, Dorsey left the board of directors of the social network.[^62]

In October 2022, Dorsey retained his 2.4% ownership of Twitter when the company was sold to [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk").[^63]

### Block

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Jack_Dorsey_in_Washington_D.C._-_2018_%28MRW_4445%29_%2844538272181%29_%28cropped-2%29.jpg/250px-Jack_Dorsey_in_Washington_D.C._-_2018_%28MRW_4445%29_%2844538272181%29_%28cropped-2%29.jpg)

Dorsey in 2018

Dorsey, along with co-founder [Jim McKelvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_McKelvey "Jim McKelvey"), developed a small business platform to accept debit and [credit card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card "Credit card") payments on a mobile device called [Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_\(financial_services\) "Square (financial services)"), released in May 2010. The small, square-shaped device attaches to [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\) "Android (operating system)"), [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad "IPad"), [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone"), or [iPod Touch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod_Touch "IPod Touch") devices via the headphone jack, and as a mini card reader, allows a person to swipe their card, choose an amount to transfer to the recipient and then sign their name for confirmation. Square is also a system for sending paperless receipts via text message or email, and is available as a free app for Android and iOS.[^64] [^65] The company grew from 10 employees in December 2009 [^66] to over 100 by June 2011. Square's office is on Market Street in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco").[^67] In September 2012, *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider "Business Insider")* magazine valued Square Inc. at [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 3.2 billion.[^68] Dorsey is CEO of Square, Inc.[^69] On October 14, 2015, Square filed for an IPO to be listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange").[^70] As of that date, Dorsey owned 24.4 percent of the company.[^71] In March 2020 the [FDIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDIC "FDIC") permitted Square to open a bank. It announced plans to launch Square Financial Services in 2021.[^72]

In May 2020, Dorsey announced that employees of Square would permanently become [remote workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_work "Remote work").[^73]

In 2020, for months, Square began withholding up to 30 percent of the funds that merchants collected from customers using its [Cash App](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_App "Cash App").[^74]

On December 1, 2021, CEO Jack Dorsey officially changed the name of the platform to Block, Inc. This was due in part to his interest in the [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain") as well as the new name encompassing the various businesses better than the current name, which is mostly associated with its merchant-payment services. The stock [ticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker_symbol "Ticker symbol") for Block, Inc. would remain "SQ".[^75]

### Bluesky

In February 2022, Dorsey joined the board of directors of [Bluesky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluesky "Bluesky"),[^76] a [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") developing a [decentralized social networking protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_network_protocol "Decentralized network protocol") and [app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_application "Web application").[^77] [^78] In May 2024, he announced he was no longer on the board of directors.[^79] [^80] [^81] He instead endorsed [X (formerly Twitter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"), which he called "freedom technology".[^82] In a 2024 interview, Dorsey said that Bluesky's shift toward a traditional corporate structure and the introduction of centralized moderation tools were major factors behind his decision to leave the company.[^83]

### Digital currency

Dorsey is one of the few persons who are speculated to be [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto "Satoshi Nakamoto"), the creator of [Bitcoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin "Bitcoin").[^84]

### Other projects

In 2013, Dorsey expressed his admiration of [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg "Michael Bloomberg") to [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN"),[^85] and said he aspires to become [mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City").[^85] He served as a judge for Bloomberg's [NYC BigApps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_BigApps "NYC BigApps") competition in 2011.[^86]

On December 24, 2013, Dorsey was announced as a new member of the board of directors of [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company").[^87] In January 2018, it was reported that Dorsey would not seek reelection at Disney's March annual meeting, due to increased difficulty with conflicts of interest.[^88]

Dorsey is a board member of the [Berggruen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berggruen_Institute "Berggruen Institute") 's Governance Center.[^2] Dorsey gives advice in a chapter of [Tim Ferriss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Ferriss "Tim Ferriss") ' book *[Tools of Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tools_of_Titans "Tools of Titans")*.[^89] [^90] [^91]

Dorsey is a vocal [Bitcoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin "Bitcoin") advocate, and has spoken at Bitcoin conferences such as "The B Word". He has said that if he were not working on Twitter and Square, he would be working on Bitcoin.[^92]

He also endorses and financially supported the development of the [Nostr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostr "Nostr") social networking protocol.[^93] [^94]

In 2023, Dorsey provided the seed funding for [OCEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCEAN_\(Bitcoin_mining_pool\) "OCEAN (Bitcoin mining pool)"), a [Bitcoin mining pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_pool "Mining pool") with the stated goal of decentralizing [Bitcoin mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_protocol "Bitcoin protocol") by giving miners the ability to select or construct their own block templates, reducing reliance on centralized [pool operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_pool "Mining pool").[^95] [^96]

In 2025, Dorsey launched a peer-to-peer messaging app called [Bitchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitchat "Bitchat"), which uses [Bluetooth mesh networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluetooth_mesh_networking "Bluetooth mesh networking") to connect nearby users without the need for an internet connection.[^97] [^98]

In November 2025, Dorsey announced he is backing a new app called diVine based on the defunct [Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_\(service\) "Vine (service)") platform.[^99]

## Personal life

In 2012, Dorsey moved to the [Sea Cliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Cliff,_San_Francisco "Sea Cliff, San Francisco") neighborhood of [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco").[^100]

### Meditation

In 2015, Dorsey said he began his mornings with [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation "Meditation").[^101] In late 2017, Dorsey completed ten days of meditation known as *[Vipassanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vipassan%C4%81 "Vipassanā")* taught by followers of [S. N. Goenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._N._Goenka "S. N. Goenka").[^102] [^103] In November 2018, Dorsey went on a birthday *Vipassanā* meditation trip to [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Myanmar "Buddhism in Myanmar").[^104]

### Politics

In 2019, Dorsey contributed financially to the campaigns of 2020 [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(United_States\) "Democratic Party (United States)") presidential candidates [Tulsi Gabbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsi_Gabbard "Tulsi Gabbard") and [Andrew Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yang "Andrew Yang").[^105] In 2020, he donated $15 million to 29 mayors pursuing the piloting of [guaranteed basic income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaranteed_basic_income "Guaranteed basic income") programs in the United States.

Until 2021, Dorsey applied "world leader" exceptions that enabled [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") to post content on Twitter that would normally be removed or generate sanctions per the platform's rules. In May 2020, some of Trump's tweets received warning labels, and from Election Day 2020, more flaggings were applied to his tweets.[^106] On January 6, 2021, after Trump supporters [stormed the U.S. Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol "2021 storming of the United States Capitol"), Twitter applied a 12-hour timeout to [Trump's account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter_use_by_Donald_Trump "Twitter use by Donald Trump") for violating its Civic Integrity policy. Trump's account was suspended permanently on January 8 based on a review of his posts' content and context.[^107] On January 14, Dorsey defended banning Trump but also said it "sets a precedent I feel is dangerous".[^108]

In June 2023, Dorsey endorsed [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Jr. "Robert F. Kennedy Jr.") in his campaign to become the Democratic party nominee in the [2024 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election").[^109] [^110] [^111]

In 2024, Dorsey tweeted in support of [Gaza war protests in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_in_the_United_States "Gaza war protests in the United States"), [Vietnam war protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_United_States_involvement_in_the_Vietnam_War "Opposition to United States involvement in the Vietnam War"), and [Iraq war protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_war_protests "Iraq war protests").[^112]

## Philanthropic and other donations

In March 2016, Dorsey fully funded about 600 Missouri public school projects registered at [DonorsChoose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DonorsChoose "DonorsChoose").[^113]

In October 2019, Dorsey donated $350,000 to [#TeamTrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Trees "Team Trees"), a nonprofit started by YouTubers [MrBeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MrBeast "MrBeast") and [Mark Rober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rober "Mark Rober"), that pledged to plant 20 million trees by the end of 2019.[^114] [^115]

On April 7, 2020, Dorsey announced that he would move about $1 billion of his equity in [Square, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square,_Inc. "Square, Inc."), just under a third of his total wealth, to Start Small, LLC, and to relief programs related to the coronavirus.[^116] [^117] [^118] He committed to funding [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") relief, girls' education and health, and [universal basic income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income "Universal basic income").[^119] Dorsey has donated $24 million to over 40 different grantees for relief efforts.[^120]

In August 2020, Dorsey donated $10 million to [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University") 's Center for Antiracist Research, founded by [Ibram X. Kendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibram_X._Kendi "Ibram X. Kendi").[^121]

In May 2021, Dorsey donated $15 million to support relief efforts in [India's COVID-19 second wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_India#2021:_Second_wave "COVID-19 pandemic in India"). The three [NGOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGO "NGO") were [Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Care_International "Care International") ($10 million), [Aid India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_India's_Development "Association for India's Development") ($2.5 million), and Sewa International ($2.5 million).[^122]

In January 2024, Dorsey gave $10 million to the [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_Press_Foundation "Freedom of the Press Foundation"), the largest donation in that organization's history.[^123] It followed a $1 million donation he had given in July 2023 in honor of whistleblower [Daniel Ellsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ellsberg "Daniel Ellsberg"), who had just died.[^124]

## Awards and recognition

- In 2008, he was named to the *[MIT Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Technology_Review "MIT Technology Review")* [TR35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TR35 "TR35") as one of the top 35 innovators in the world under the age of 35.[^125]
- In 2012, *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* gave him the "Innovator of the Year Award" for technology.[^126] [^127]
- At the 5th Annual Crunchies Awards in 2012, hosted by [TechCrunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechCrunch "TechCrunch"), Dorsey was named Founder of the Year.[^128]
- In 2013, he was considered by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* the world's most eligible billionaire bachelor.[^129]
- Dorsey was ranked by [Fox Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Business_Network "Fox Business Network") as the No. 4 Worst CEO of 2016, citing stagnant growth, falling stock prices, and his part-time commitment to Twitter.[^130]
- In 2017, *24/7 Wall St.* listed Dorsey among the 20 Worst CEOs in America.[^131] [^132]
