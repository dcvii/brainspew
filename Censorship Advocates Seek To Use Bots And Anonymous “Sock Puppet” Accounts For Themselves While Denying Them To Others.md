### Democrats both used and demanded censorship of bots — and funded R&D into them. Now, Australia’s governments want to use bots in violation of the policies of social media platforms.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!hJ_R!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0d9a9616-e7dc-4171-ad02-139094b11144_2870x1308.png)

Anika Wells, Australia’s Minister for Sport and Communications, on September 8, 2026 (left). Australia’s Prime Minister Anthony Albanese with Wells on August 15, 2022 (center); eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant (right) on November 5, 2025 (GETTY IMAGES)

Political leaders across the West have said for a decade that governments must end online anonymity to shut down fake accounts known as bots, particularly those from Russia. “I want to see real names on the internet,” [said](https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/news/friedrich-merz-fordert-klarnamen-im-internet-aber-die-umsetzung-bleibt-offen-li.10019960) German Chancellor Friedrich Merz in February. “In a democracy, citizens have the right to privacy,” [said](https://www.weforum.org/stories/global-cooperation/davos-2025-special-address-pedro-sanchez-prime-minister-spain/) Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez at Davos in January 2025, and “not to anonymity or impunity,” proposing to tie every social media account to a European digital identity wallet. Greece’s digital governance minister [proposed](https://greekreporter.com/2026/04/29/greece-end-anonymous-social-media-accounts/) in April 2026 to link every profile to a verified identity because “The major problem behind anonymity is toxicity.” French President Emmanuel Macron [declared](https://www.elysee.fr/emmanuel-macron/2025/12/10/face-aux-lecteurs-de-ouest-france) that his priority was “to demand the disappearance of fake accounts” and “these famous bots, these troll farms.”

But now the advocates of government censorship of legal speech are demanding the legal power to wage information warfare through anonymous accounts and an explicit “sock puppet” strategy. Buried in the Australian government’s [new censorship bill](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/exposure-draft-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026-september2026.pdf) is a definition: “A sock puppet identity is a false or fictitious identity assumed by a person in the course of using, or carrying out any activity in relation to, an online service.” The legislation goes on not to warn of bad actors using sock puppets but rather to authorize the eSafety Commissioner, Julie Inman Grant, to create and use sock puppets to “disseminate” information.

Michael Nayna and Andrew Lowenthal [first reported](https://x.com/naffects/status/2104412536745627703?s=46) on the sock puppet provisions last week.
