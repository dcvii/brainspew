On May 19, 2026, Robert Woodson passed away. In his long career, which stretched from his work with the NAACP in the 1960s through heading up his own flourishing institution, [the Woodson Center](https://woodsoncenter.org/), Bob maintained a nearly single-minded focus on black communities, black families, and black people. When I met Bob in the 1980s, he was an astonishingly energetic advocate for the potential and capacities of his people. Before and during the Civil Rights Era, he witnessed firsthand what black people could accomplish even while enduring threats to their lives and the deprivation of basic rights.

He saw black Americans building thriving businesses, healthy communities, and stable families. He saw them stepping up to assert their rights and accrue political power. As he was fond of saying, “When white people were at their worst, we were at our best.”

That belief in the capacity of black Americans to do for themselves drove him. It was the source of his prodigious energy, which he used to advocate for community-based initiatives from the street to the highest levels of government. Indeed, one of my early ventures with Bob was a 1986 meeting at the White House, where the two of us and a number of other black leaders met with Ronald Reagan, George H.W. Bush, and other officials to discuss ideas for creating opportunity within black communities.

Last week, the American Enterprise Institute hosted a tribute to the life and work of Bob Woodson. I was not able to attend, but I contributed a short remembrance of Bob, which was read beautifully by Assistant Secretary for Civil Rights at the U.S. Department of Agriculture Devon Westhill.
