President Trump went after the Ford Class again. Speaking at the Pennsylvania Defense Innovation Summit in Carlisle on July 15 — his third public attack on the program — Trump railed against EMALS, the island design, and the advanced weapons elevators, took a shot at Huntington-Ingalls Industries, and declared no future carriers would be built with electromagnetic catapults. He also threatened, not entirely in jest, to fire Pete Hegseth if they were. Mooch digs into what’s actually driving the frustration, what the realistic options are for Ford Class construction going forward, and what the U.S. Navy actually needs from its carrier fleet in the decades ahead.

Two encores provide the full arc of this recurring fight. The first traces Trump’s EMALS obsession from his 2017 visit to CVN-78 through the USS Wasp poll and the Thanksgiving phone call with USS Ronald Reagan CO Capt. Pat Hannifan — and then assesses the four new Ford technologies (EMALS, AAG, the new island, and the new reactors) on their merits. The second encore covers Trump’s order to the Navy aboard USS George Washington at Yokosuka to return to steam catapults, whether that’s operationally feasible, and the two aircraft that crashed off USS Nimitz within thirty minutes of each other due to bad fuel.

## Video Roundup

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kPYPGtmR1Wg)

At the Pennsylvania Defense Innovation Summit on July 15, President Trump went off-script for the third time to attack the Ford Class carrier program — hitting EMALS, the island design, and the advanced weapons elevators, taking aim at Huntington-Ingalls Industries, and declaring no future carriers would be built with electromagnetic catapults. He also threatened to fire SecDef Pete Hegseth if they were — and wasn’t entirely joking.

EMALS is clearly something Trump won’t let go. But what are the realities of Ford Class construction, and what does the U.S. Navy actually need going forward? Mooch explores all of it in this special livestream.

---

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0g2enjnzxQY)

**ENCORE:** Trump’s EMALS fixation started in 2017 when a Ford crew member complained about the catapults during a presidential visit — and he ran with it, polling the crew of USS Wasp (an amphib, not a carrier) and calling for a return to steam. It appeared to end when USS Ronald Reagan CO Capt. Pat Hannifan told Trump on a Thanksgiving call that he preferred digital cats because of the burden of “chasing steam around the ship.” The President said he liked that answer.

But the controversy never really went away, because the problems with CVN-78 are real and documented. In this encore, Ward assesses all four of the Ford’s new technologies — EMALS, AAG, the new island, and the new reactors — and asks the question that actually matters: will the effort be worth it?

---

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zzf5GRX-t5g)

**ENCORE:** During an hour-long speech to the crew of the USS George Washington (CVN 73) docked at Yokosuka Naval Base in Japan, President Trump told the U.S. Navy leaders present, including a four-star admiral, that he was ordering them to stop building aircraft carriers with electromagnetic catapults currently in use on Ford class and to return to steam catapults used by Nimitz class.  
  
In this livestream episode Mooch discusses if this is feasible and what it could mean to the future of the U.S. Navy's aircraft carrier inventory, already under stress because of the issues behind the construction of the Ford class.
