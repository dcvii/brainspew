#### 5\. REPORTER’S NOTEBOOK

**Rachel Louise Ensign:** Earlier this year, my colleague Paul Overberg and I wanted to look at whether middle-class families with children are leaving New York City. Paul, the newsroom’s expert on demographics, compiled and analyzed a decade of census data. We quickly saw that this was [a phenomenon that extended far beyond New York](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/cities-losing-families-children-a860a9b1?mod=article_inline). Cities, rich and poor, in every geographic corner of the country, were seeing big declines in kids. The decreases were even more stark for little children (in San Jose, for instance, kids under 5 are down a staggering 34%).

Our analysis looked at the 38 U.S. cities with more than 500,000 people, so I interviewed families across the country to learn about what was happening in their communities. I spoke to many families who found high-cost places like New York too expensive and chose to move. But even families living in cheaper areas worried about the rising cost of living after years of rising home prices and inflation on everyday goods.

The falling birthrate is also a huge factor here, so I interviewed people who don’t have children, who really helped me see how nuanced this all is. One woman talked about how she decided to not do IVF in large part because of the costs involved. Another told me about how she’s not having kids because that would mean giving up activities she enjoys, plus she wants to be able to help support her mother and stepfather if they need it in the future.

The big declines in kids have changed the social fabric of cities across the country and are already hitting local public school systems hard. We’re planning to keep digging into this fascinating topic.

The price of [this summer’s status symbol](https://www.wsj.com/style/loonen-glass-bottled-water-microplastics-97c5d6f8?mod=article_inline): a glass bottle of water. From the shelves of bougie Los Angeles grocer Erewhon to private events at clubs like Casa Cipriani, Loonen water has become the chosen hydration of the chic, affluent and health-conscious. Its glass carafes appeal to eco-anxious consumers who want to eradicate microplastics from their lives. And as water filtration has become an obsession of the rich, the brand’s stainless-steel process has helped it market the bottles—where at some stores they cost nearly $6—to an upscale clientele.

### River cruises are a snooze and fans wouldn’t want it any other way.
