**How University of Denver faculty use debate to foster constructive disagreement across the curriculum.** Since 2022, a faculty-built program has brought more than 250 structured debates into University of Denver classrooms. Treating debate as a shared search for truth rather than a contest, with no winners and randomly assigned sides, it has produced gains in topic knowledge, comfort speaking up, and intellectual humility. And you can bring the program to your classroom. ## For Your Eyes and Ears

When does philosophical inquiry cross an ethical line? Some critics say certain views should be silenced for the harm they might cause. In this HxA research colloquium, Rebecca Tuvel (Rhodes College) presents the “Institutional Model” she developed with Eric Sampson (Purdue University). This model argues that philosophers need to be free to advance views that turn out to be mistaken, or even unintentionally harmful, in service of the discipline’s aim of advancing understanding. A view should be silenced on harm grounds only when there is excellent evidence of imminent harm.

## Something Extra

The [Buckley Institute’s 2026 National Undergraduate Student Survey](https://buckleyinstitute.com/2026-national-undergraduate-survey/?utm_campaign=Weekly%20Bulletin%20Emails%20FY25&utm_source=hs_email&utm_medium=email&utm_content=443781910&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-80ptxj1JxRPfD1MKR7P7XmqzTa_p5DGKbTLswx0_WAyjMmODnXYednPp_G45Xvm02KtcA0csX2o2A13iSTYb1tAg7_Eg) of full-time four-year undergraduates, fielded in September, found that a majority (54%) have often felt intimidated in sharing their ideas, opinions, or beliefs in class because they were different from those of their professors (12% frequently, 42% sometimes). That is essentially unchanged from last year’s 53%. Another 44% said not often (25% rarely, 19% never). By ideology, 55% of liberal students, 54% of moderates, and 47% of conservatives said often.
