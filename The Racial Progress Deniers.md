Last week’s election shattered myriad myths, not least that America is “structurally” racist. Minority candidates across the country won races up and down the ballot. After his proclaimed victory this week, Democrat Scott Colom will be [Mississippi](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/mississippi) ’s first black Senator since the post-Civil War Reconstruction era.

Mr. Colom edged GOP incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith, which will give Democrats at least a 55-seat Senate majority. Democrats are hailing the victory as historic, and they’re right. Mississippi favored President Trump by 23 points in 2024 and hasn’t elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1982. The state’s last Democratic Senator, John Stennis, supported segregation until his later years.

Relatively high black turnout and a backlash to inflation helped Mr. Colom, though he notably also won a majority of white independents. Democrats claim that little has changed in the South since Jim Crow. This has long been the essence of their argument that racial gerrymandering is needed to increase minority representation in Congress.

This conceit also underlies their criticism of the Supreme Court’s landmark *Shelby County* (2013) decision, which effectively nullified a Voting Rights Act provision that required many states and localities to get federal approval for new electoral maps and voting law changes. We were told *Shelby* would augur a return to Jim Crow, but the day hasn’t come.

Minority turnout has since increased in many states and even sometimes surpassed that of whites. Meantime, more minorities are winning election in states with white majorities. Several states last week elected their first black governors, including Georgia (Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms), Florida (Republican Byron Donalds), Nevada (Democrat Aaron Ford) and Wisconsin (Democrat David Crowley).

Gina Hinojosa will be the first Hispanic Governor of Texas. Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed will become the Senate’s first Muslim. The anti-Israel Council on American-Islamic Relations reported last week that a record 97 (and perhaps more) Muslims were elected nationwide at the federal, state and local levels. So much for Islamophobia.

Alas, the political left doesn’t want to acknowledge that voting has become far less racially polarized. To the extent that whites favor white candidates, it’s because white voters are more likely to be Republicans and so are the candidates they are voting for. Mr. Donalds won 58% of white voters, and Mr. Crowley took 51% of them.

All of this further exposes the anachronism of the High Court’s *Gingles* test for proving vote dilution under the Voting Rights Act. This test requires that a racial majority votes sufficiently as a bloc to usually defeat the minority group’s preferred candidate. The Justices largely did away with this test in their *Louisiana v. Callais* decision this year, and Democrats reacted with fury and more cries of Jim Crow.

Why won’t Democrats celebrate America’s manifest racial progress?
