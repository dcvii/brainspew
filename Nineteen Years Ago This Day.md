I write this on Sunday afternoon, July 26, 2026, after having beclowned myself at the sporting clays range on yet another beautiful Sunday morning. There is a real contest right now between which retirement practice I am worse at, sporting clays or golf. In either case, every time I step up to do what is required, I feel like I have never before done what I am about to do. I hope that with more practice (in both) this feeling will dissipate.

Nineteen years ago this day (July 26, 2007) started as any other day would have at that point in my uneasy life. I was in my last summer in the Navy, my retirement request having recently been approved, and I had no real sense of what I would be doing on April 1st 2008 when I would no longer be accepting the crown’s coin. I was dating here and there, but there were no known threats to long-term solitude. I had recently begun to talk on the telephone (after a few emails back and forth showcasing my jaunty wit) with a woman who was geographically undesirable, but who had an awfully cute giggle. She had been described to me by a mutual friend as “a beautiful young widow who lives on the water surrounded by books”, and two days later, I was scheduled to drive out to her Eastern Shore locale to meet her for dinner (a Saturday evening). I decided to get a hotel room for that night so that I could get up early the next morning and drive home rather than doing so after dinner.

I left the Pentagon that day (Thursday July 26) and headed home to change before meeting up with a friend of mine for dinner, a retired Navy captain who was now working in the defense industry. This was what the retirement seminars referred to as a “networking” dinner, and I was making the rounds of friends and mentors who could give me some advice as to how to make the transition from Navy to civilian.

About halfway through dinner, I got a text message on my pocket sized Nokia from “the giggler” that said something like “What are the chances that I would run into you at McGarvey’s in Annapolis tonight at 9PM?”. I showed my friend the message and he asked what I thought of her. I told him she’s really smart and has a great voice and an awesome giggle, but she lives an hour and forty-five minutes away WITHOUT DC TRAFFIC. I also told him I had not met her yet, but that I really wanted to. He said, “why not?”. So I texted her that there was a fine chance she would run into me.

Where it all began

After dinner, I hopped in my car and drove out to McGarvey’s and stationed myself across from the entrance. I had decided that I liked her a good deal by this point, and that she had only to be not unattractive for there to be reason enough for me to be interested. Then she walked in.

God’s honest truth, I was blown away. Not just because she was good looking (which she was), but because something inside me clicked. I had had a good deal of literarily suffused emails back and forth with her, and a few long phone chats, and through these channels I had come to realize that this was a really serious woman, a widow raising two daughters on her own who didn’t have time for unserious men. And I am nothing if not unserious.

I leapt out of my chair when she walked in and made the first of countless mistakes. I gave her a big kiss on the cheek. She was a bit flummoxed by this, and she quickly maneuvered a table for two between us to return to her some semblance of personal space. I was SKYING! As I sat there realizing that Catherine was likely going to be in my life for a long, long, time, Catherine was sitting there wondering who this crazed lunatic is who accosted her upon entry. Seriously. It all could have ended right there on the spot, but in the first instance of her infinite patience, she gave me time to redeem myself.

I walked her to her car at about 0100 and began my drive back to Arlington, knowing I would have an utterly grueling day at the Pentagon ahead of me that would start at about 0700. A few minutes after I got underway, the phone rang. It was HER! She did like me! She wouldn’t call right away to keep her company on our drives (hers was about as far as mine) if she didn’t!. We had a lovely chat, and sometime after two AM, I began my three and a half hours of sleep before a busy Friday.

On Friday afternoon, I left early, around 3PM. As I walked out toward Pentagon South Parking on a brutally hot afternoon to begin the Bataan death march to my apartment in Crystal City, my cellphone got coverage and displayed a text message from Catherine. “How would you like to attend the Youth Sailing Auction with me at the Yacht Club tonight?” I’d never been a a yacht club before, and visions of Judge Smails danced in my head. Honestly, milliseconds before reading this, all I wanted was the air conditioned comfort of my apartment, the wall-to wall carpeting under my feet, and a giant bag of Chinese food from the place down the street. I would eat until I fell asleep, and it would be a glorious Friday night.

But remember, everything had changed. There was now a SOMEBODY. That SOMEBODY seemed as interested in me as I was in her. Keep in mind, we had a proper date SCHEDULED for the next night and I had a hotel room. I quickly called the hotel and asked if I could add the present night and they said “sure, come on out.” So I texted her my plans and drove out for the first time to her geographically undesirable location fueled by 7-11 coffee applied liberally to my exhaustion. She picked me up at my hotel in her giant Toyota Land Cruiser and we headed out to the auction.

The next morning I awoke to the sound of my phone ringing. I was in a strange place, and it was pitch dark. I looked at the phone, and it said it was a little after 11AM. It was Catherine, asking if I’d like to go to the park and have an ice cream cone. I was utterly disoriented, I had only just realized where I was, but instinct kicked in and I said yes, give me thirty minutes.

After the park and ice cream, I went back to my hotel for a serious nap to try and recapture some of the sleep lost over the two previous nights. What you just read is how I fell in love and how I had three dates with that woman before our first date ever really happened.

Almost nothing about my life is the same as it was before that day. And that…is a very good thing.
