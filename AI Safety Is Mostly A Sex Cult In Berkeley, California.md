These people shouldn’t make policy.

There has been a great effort over many years to distance AI Safety discussion from its origin and intellectual center, because its center is a sex cult started by a fan fiction author. Since those involved want to be taken seriously, they have to hide that. Everyone else working in AI Safety is, in effect, a smokescreen for this core group and their ideas.

We might some day have “AI Safety”, as a science or policy area, that is not primarily a sex cult in Berkeley, California, but it does not currently exist.

Yudkowsky is sufficiently controversial that people motivated by his ideas often downplay or deny his influence. Sometimes this spans multiple layers: People who received money from Sam Bankman-Fried denying they’re Effective Altruists, Sam Bankman-Fried denying he’s an Effective Altruist when caught, Effective Altruists denying their version of AI Safety is heavily influenced by Yudkowsky’s, people who are extremely core members of the movement often call themselves “rationalist adjacent”, and so on. But Yudkowsky is provably, publicly influential on essentially all major public figures in AI. It is easy to deny not because it is hidden but because it seems so insane.

## Who Are The Rationalists?

Yudkowsky’s more dedicated followers seem to prefer to call themselves “the rationality community” these days, everyone else calls them “rationalists”, the entire following “rationalism”. “Rats” is also common.[^4]

This is a long denial that [they are a religion](https://segyges.github.io/posts/is-rationalism-a-religion/). I alternate between finding these denials funny and finding them insulting.

![Three-slide carousel of a post by Aella, @Aella_Girl. Slide 1: “ok full thoughts on 'is rationality religion' now that i have a keyboard and time. 1. Where there is any separation between the two things in the Bay, “rationalism” is people and things more closely tied to Yudkowsky, and “effective altruism” has had a few institutional centers but is currently focused around Anthropic and its CEO, Dario Amodei. They compete for attention and funding in that space. Yudkowsky’s position is that AI will definitely kill us, and Dario’s position is that AI is [ten to twenty-five percent likely to kill us](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/16/ai-doom-risk-anthropic-openai-google).

Both are saying what justifies giving them and no one else money, labor and attention. Since Yudkowsky is a writer and not a scientist of any kind, he can only claim that he and his organization are important and deserve money and prestige if AI Safety is an unsolvable problem and only advocating against AI matters. Since Dario leads a company that actually makes AI, Dario must claim that “AI doom” is a very large problem that only his company is able to manage.

Yudkowsky, similarly, claimed that his (non-profit) company was the best chance at “saving the human species”, back when AI companies seldom made anything and he could claim to be doing research by simply writing.[^6] Yudkowsky’s belief system is a successful [pitch](https://intelligence.org/files/strategicplan2011.pdf) for investors, [donors](https://intelligence.org/2014/04/02/2013-in-review-fundraising/), [volunteers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WfB4REtXjJgo6K8Sz/call-for-help-volunteers-needed-to-proofread-miri-s) and [employees](https://intelligence.org/2018/11/22/2018-update-our-new-research-directions/). It is also, politically and doctrinally, a pitch for people to give you total control over them, because if they do not, the world might end. After Yudkowsky refined this pitch modifications of it became the pitch for organizations like OpenAI and Anthropic [^7]. This millenarian ideology appears to have become integral to employee morale and recruiting because so many research staff are believers to one degree or another.

Yudkowsky is not going to stop preaching the gospel of doom because, among other reasons, doing so is the only job he has ever had. He would stand to lose his social position and the [roughly $600,000 in income](https://intelligence.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/990-2024.pdf#page=7) he is paid out of his non-profit if he changed his tune. In general, if doom prophecies are worth money, people will produce more. This system’s purpose is to attract funding and young idealists and to find ways to spend the funding and make use of the young idealists. Historically, rationalists have seemed to encourage many of their young idealists to take up sex work.[^8]

People tend to follow their incentives.

## From EA To Anthropic, To Policy

People sometimes deny that Anthropic is an Effective Altruist organization. This may be technically true. Anthropic, the company, can say it is or isn’t whatever it wants to. Anthropic’s founders, senior employees, and investors, however, have numerous ties with Effective Altruism.[^9]

Dario in particular is almost perfectly on-type for a Bay Area Effective Altruist in what he says and how he says it. Dario’s often bizarre, vague, and meandering writing style alone has no real precedent besides LessWrong and its AI-focused spinoff, Alignment Forum. He has been directly involved in Yudkowsky’s community meetups since at least 2008.[^10] Anthropic’s strategy is very similar to [the playbook Yudkowsky laid out](https://intelligence.org/files/AIFoomDebate.pdf), back before the success of deep learning and companies that specialize in deep learning made Yudkowsky’s organization no longer credible as a serious research project.

This same denial of affiliation happens when it comes to the incestuous relationships between all of the other non-profits in the Bay that are meant to be focused on AI risk. They virtually all draw support from a handful of sources of funding and they recruit from the movement. Holden Karnofsky, currently Anthropic, formerly head of Coefficient Giving, was [an original board member of Redwood Research](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/JZqbNjtAqieivTG7Q/redwood-research-is-hiring-for-several-roles) and [one of the initial champions for METR](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/holden-karnofsky-how-ai-could-take-over-the-world/). Both Redwood and METR hold themselves up as independent organizations. They may be formally independent, but they are aimed at the same goals as the rest of the movement and are broadly ideologically compliant.

Redwood Research in particular seems to be trying to catch the same lightning in a bottle that Yudkowsky had with MIRI while avoiding the sloppiness of his presentation or tying the whole thing to his specific social life. Redwood seems to be laundering essentially the same world view into policy papers that can be cited and pointed to as having come from a serious organization. Their CEO is a former MIRI staffer [^11], and when they first launched they had a glowing quote from Nate Soares, who has been either Executive Director or President of MIRI since 2015, on their site.[^12]

Because the Bay Area Rationalists and Effective Altruists are so influential in the AI industry, people who are not insiders to this subculture cannot meaningfully influence the research or policy agenda of that industry. This includes many of the people doing actual research. Some current AI Safety research may produce useful knowledge for the future, but its main effect today is to lend scientific legitimacy to people who are not at all doing science, but who are asserting a very particular religious and political agenda.

## Paperclips, Doom

Yudkowsky’s most viral idea, the one that infects the most people and spreads the farthest, is that AI, if we continue to develop it, will

1. **certainly** recursively self-improve (“RSI”), quickly achieving superintelligence (“ASI”),
2. **certainly** escape human control,
3. **certainly** kill everyone (“paperclip them”, “doom”).

All arguments about AI Safety and AI Alignment are primarily about mitigating this exact scenario under his exact terms. Significant modification is relatively rare. Where lower probabilities of doom than “certain” are given, people seem to still generally worry exclusively about Yudkowsky’s rough scenario: a sudden, unpredictable sequence of 1) superintelligence, 2) immediate escape from control, and 3) the death of all humans on Earth. This is always, implicitly, what "safety" and "alignment" are about.

These ideas occasionally veer into the belief that AI is magic. When presented as serious science and policy, this sort of thing is omitted.

### 90s-00s: Usenet, Transhumanism, Extropianism

In 1996, a 17-year-old Eliezer Yudkowsky joins Usenet and the Extropians mailing list. His more notable influences are Drexler’s book on nanotechnology, Kurzweil’s book on AI, and Nick Bostrom, who frequents the Extropians mailing list. (Usenet was still largely full of academics). In 1998, Nick Bostrom [argues that a superintelligent AI might not necessarily be good or friendly](https://diyhpl.us/~bryan/irc/extropians/extracted-extropians-archive/archive/9812/29988.html), which, along with many of Bostrom’s other ideas, will later become core to Yudkowsky.

In the year 2000, Yudkowsky founds the Singularity Institute for Artificial Intelligence, SIAI, later renamed to the Machine Intelligence Research Institute, MIRI. This appears to have started as a fake-it-til-you-make-it sort of thing, and it doesn’t pick up until he gets sponsored by Peter Thiel. Peter Thiel will be his largest sponsor until about 2015 or 2016. Now in 2026, Thiel has long since cut all ties, called this scene a [“burning man camp” and “Luddite”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ibR_ULHYirs&t=1571s), and called Yudkowsky the Antichrist.[^14]

Jeffrey Epstein gave them [fifty thousand dollars in 2009](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qqhdj3W3vSfB5E9ss/siai-an-examination). One of MIRI’s former associates, Ben Goertzel, [appears to have solicited a separate $2,000 donation from Epstein](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00774429.pdf) that went in under his name in that year, also. ([Confirmed separately on his blog](https://bengoertzel.substack.com/p/goertzel-vs-epstein)). One of the former presidents of MIRI is mentioned in Jeffrey Epstein’s emails [^15], which pass on thanks for a visit to the island. (MIRI is documented as turning down his money in 2016 when he was much more notorious, [if you want to figure out what to make of their statements about that](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3JjKWWrKWJ8nysD9r/question-about-a-past-donor-to-miri).)

### 2009-2015: Writing, Community-Building

Eliezer Yudkowsky spends some time before 2009 blogging, and then contracts out setting up LessWrong as a customized version of Reddit’s code. In 2009, he bundles his last year or two of blog posts and puts them all on the newly made LessWrong, where they come to be called The Sequences. Much later, in 2015, they are published as *Rationality: From AI to Zombies*. This is a series of essays about science and epistemology, most of them pretty closely related to books and ideas then in the zeitgeist but with both a very distinctly transhumanist and distinctly AI Doom thesis. (So, “conquer death with technology” and “if we do technology wrong, we are all going to die”.) It is written in such a way that it also works as, effectively, a self-help book about how to be better at thinking. This will later be something like the Rationalist Old Testament.

From 2010 to 2015, he writes HPMOR, and his audience becomes much larger, and this becomes a well-defined scene and people invested in the movement begin relocating to the Bay Area.

In 2010, Roko Mijic invents Roko’s Basilisk, which is most easily explained as the invention of Rationalist Pascal’s Wager, Rationalist Satan and Rationalist Hell, where those who fail to evangelize and help bring about the eschaton are sent. This will seem, over time, to drive the rationalists who are the most susceptible to religious mania and obsession insane.[^16] Yudkowsky disowns the Basilisk immediately and tries to ban discussion of it from LessWrong, which makes it more popular.

At least as late as 2013, Yudkowsky is still advocating that we should make AI as long as we could do it safely.[^17] He is under the impression that his non-profit, MIRI, might be able to make “Friendly AI”, that is, one that does not kill everyone, ushers in the singularity and makes them immortal.

The Center for Applied Rationality, or CFAR, is [founded in 2011](https://rationality.org/files/cfar-2022-990.pdf) and offers classes. You essentially pay money for self-help, but it is primarily a [recruiting front for MIRI,](https://intelligence.org/2015/07/18/targets-1-and-2-growing-miri/) Yudkowsky’s actual operation. It also seems to function as a more-official avenue for community management, and its newsletters describe it [coordinating co-housing meetings](https://rationality.org/resources/updates/2017/second-quarter-cfar-newsletter) and [mediating alumni disputes](https://rationality.org/resources/updates/2017/fourth-quarter-cfar-newsletter).

They start a bunch of children’s camps, math camps, and study programs that are mostly still around. SPARC was the earliest, [beginning in 2012](https://acritch.com/sparc/), and takes high school students. All of its graduates from then will be adults now. Probably a bunch of them live in the Bay and work on AI safety.

Your bottom tier are people who are very young, are not particularly skilled, who do not fit in enough, or who otherwise do not thrive in the Bay’s job and housing market. [^32]: From Yudkowsky’s writing here, the solution must meet the following criteria:

- Prevent competing AGI projects, big and small, present and future.
- Be less difficult than locating and destroying every GPU.
- Require capabilities that advanced AI could supply.
- Be sufficiently taboo that he deliberately avoids describing it in public.

For the first point: This piece argues that progressively smaller and smaller actors will become capable of building AGI over time. Given that assumption, stopping the current laboratories does not solve the problem, because other people can easily replace them; you must stop *almost anyone, even small groups of people,* from attempting to create AGI.

For the second point: We are told that locating and destroying all of the relevant hardware is *somewhat more difficult* than what you should actually do. From this, we can infer that the intended plan is *somewhat less technologically difficult* than that.

In particular, this rules out any plan concerning the use of political power. Achieving regulatory control worldwide over all GPUs or all of research would pretty clearly be more difficult than just destroying the GPUs. Maintaining the coercion and surveillance necessary to keep a monopoly over all GPUs indefinitely is substantively more difficult than simply destroying the GPUs! Destroying the GPUs would be at most as difficult as maintaining such vast political power, since you could simply use such a monopoly on violence to destroy all of the GPUs, and all other relevant hardware, and really anything else you wanted.

For the third point: He directly says that this requires a relatively powerful AGI. Whatever the obstacle is, being much smarter must help you overcome it; it must involve some problem of knowledge, design, or engineering. This, too, rules out ordinary regulatory, military, diplomatic or political means. Simply acquiring political authority would not explain why you needed an AGI to execute this “pivotal act”.

For the fourth point: He explicitly says that “all known pivotal acts are currently outside the Overton Window”. He explicitly says that he chose burning all GPUs as an example because he *does not* advocate for it, allowing him to discuss the need for a pivotal act without explicitly advocating any particular plan. We are being directly told that what he thinks people actually should or will do is “currently outside the Overton Window”, and that he is *refusing to directly state* what “pivotal act” he thinks should be done.

Taken together, these constraints rule out any plausible reading other than engineered biological weapons.

We can also assume he picked this example because it is similar to the plan he does not want to say out loud. It is meant as a deliberate hint.

His chosen example is building nanotechnology that can seek, recognize, and destroy GPUs. This is presented as “needlessly difficult to align”.

We can note that “align” in this case is a type of weapons targeting. This clause specifically hints that the *real* plan makes targeting easier.

Why would you choose this example to hint at, but not directly state, some other plan? What specific plan does this hint at most directly?

Designing a suitable biological weapon for killing those working or potentially working on rival projects fits perfectly. Humans are generally easier to find and to harm than machines. An engineered disease naturally spreads from victim to victim, and naturally tends to harm them. It would be easier to “align” such a weapon to remove the people who represent a threat, and the problem of creating such a weapon is similar to the problem of creating something that would destroy only GPUs but is somewhat easier.

That is the plan this written passage is meant to indicate. Nobody I have talked to can give me another interpretation that even remotely fits here. It is simply too upsetting for people to believe this is actually what he meant, even though it’s not very different or much worse than openly advocating in favor of increasing the risk of nuclear war, which he is known to have done openly in Time Magazine.
