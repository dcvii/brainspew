The plans had been in the works for some time. Maybe, possibly, a year or two. No one told me because I am a writer and, you know, we writers cannot keep secrets. Two days ago, my wife reminded me of a family obligation planned for yesterday. My daughter was flying into town as were family members of my younger son’s girlfriend. I did not know what to expect but I knew the destination. I left home yesterday at around 1:30 p.m. I had nearly forgotten. Nikole Hannah-Jones had thrown me for such a loop.

I parked my car in Balboa Park and walked to the Marston House. I was a little anxious as I always am when I enter social events. What was the setup? What was the game plan? What were we doing? I was left in the dark on purpose it seemed. I met Don and Laurie and four siblings whose names I could not remember to save my soul. They were all polite and excited and exuded Southern charm. God, I miss Southerners at times!

I learned we were going to take a tour of the Marston House. Ok, mine was not to question why. The Marston House is named after a famous entrepreneur from the San Diego past.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xS4M0KuLbwg)

The tour guide was rushed as if he knew the plan. I did not know the plan. An asymmetrical relationship for sure. The House was fascinating. I enjoyed the details and heirlooms and black and white family pictures. It was also odd how turbocharged his speech was.

Remember Don and Laurie? I tried very hard to be pleasant and witty with them. They were the parents of my younger son’s girlfriend. As we reached the top floor of the House, pieces of the puzzle fell into place. *Where are they? Where are they? My cell says they are closing in.* You see, my Bright and Morning Star was taking autumn pictures of my younger son and his girlfriend on the grounds. We all went out onto the balcony and watched the three of them together—M., C., Bright and Morning Star. By the way, my daughter is a boss photographer. She was in the flow.

As we all stood on the balcony, close friends of my younger son’s girlfriend diplomatically cleared the grounds of other people. It was a marvelous thing to behold as total strangers understood. The close friends hid in bushes. I could see all of this drama unfolding from the balcony of the House. Someone said, *Shush!* My daughter positioned the couple throughout the garden as her camera clicked and clicked and clicked. C. never looked up. If she had, she would have seen Mom, Dad, and four siblings who should have been living their lives in North Carolina at this moment.

I heard a shriek from down below. It was C. While my daughter clicked away like a *Glamour Magazine* professional, my younger son bent down on one knee and proposed marriage to C. She must have said YES! as she let out a scream that we all could hear from the House balcony. Then, C.’s close friends stood up and screamed. Then, we all screamed with joy from the House balcony. Mom, Dad, Future Dad-in-Law, Future Mom-in-Law, Grandma/Future Grandma-in-Law, Sisters and Brother and Brother/Future Brother-in-Law, Husband and Girlfriend of Siblings—compound relationships to be untangled over time.

Two families were now becoming one family. I told this story just now to my Jewish neighbor. All he could say was ***Mazel Tov!***
